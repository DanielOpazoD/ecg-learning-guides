# 2. Territories and branch points

Which leads drop out as the clot moves

Duración indicada: 25 min

## Objetivos

- Map LAD, circumflex and RCA territories onto the 17 segments and the RV
- Use V1, aVL and V4R to locate the clot relative to S1, D1 and the RV branch
- Recognise high lateral OMI

## 1. Branch order decides the ECG

Tipo: read · Identificador: m2s1

The LAD supplies the anterior wall and septum (through septal perforators) and the anterolateral wall (through diagonals). It often wraps the apex. The circumflex supplies the lateral and inferolateral walls through obtuse marginals. The RCA supplies the RV through its marginal branch, and in most people the inferior wall and inferior septum.
A clot cuts off everything downstream. Before S1, V1 rises (septum). Before D1, I and aVL rise (high lateral wall). Before the RV branch, V1 and V4R rise (right ventricle). Move the clot past a branch and those leads fall away.

Escenario: proxLAD

## 2. Slide down the LAD

Tipo: task · Identificador: m2s2


Press the ▸ travel button on the occlusion chip and let the clot slide all the way down the LAD. Watch V1 fall after S1 and aVL after D1.

## 3. Find the RV branch

Tipo: task · Identificador: m2s3


Find an RCA position where V4R is elevated (RV involved), then move the clot so V4R is no longer elevated.

## 4. Raise the South African flag

Tipo: task · Identificador: m2s4


Without loading the preset, occlude the branch that produces STE in I, aVL and V2 with STD in III.

## 5. Territory drill

Tipo: drill · Identificador: m2s5


Answer 15 Territory items with 70% or better.

## 6. Check: territories

Tipo: check · Identificador: m2s6


### STE in V1 alongside anterior STE suggests the occlusion is:

1. Proximal to the first septal perforator
2. Distal to the second diagonal
3. In the right coronary artery
4. In the circumflex

**Respuesta del original:** Proximal to the first septal perforator

**Explicación:** S1 supplies the basal septum, which V1 faces. Losing it adds V1 elevation, so V1 STE marks a proximal LAD clot.

### STE in I, aVL and V2 with STD in III, and little change in V3–V5, points to:

1. The first diagonal (high lateral wall)
2. The distal RCA
3. A wraparound LAD
4. The left main

**Respuesta del original:** The first diagonal (high lateral wall)

**Explicación:** This is the South African flag of high lateral OMI from D1, a ramus or a high OM.

### In proximal RCA occlusion with RV infarction, V1 is typically:

1. Elevated, while V2 is depressed
2. Depressed along with V2
3. Unchanged
4. Elevated more than V3 and V4

**Respuesta del original:** Elevated, while V2 is depressed

**Explicación:** The RV vector raises V1 while the posterior component depresses V2. V4R confirms the RV involvement.

### Which territory most often produces an ECG with no diagnostic ST change?

1. Circumflex and obtuse marginals
2. Proximal LAD
3. Proximal RCA
4. Left main

**Respuesta del original:** Circumflex and obtuse marginals

**Explicación:** The lateral and inferolateral walls face away from most standard leads. Echo and posterior leads matter most here.
