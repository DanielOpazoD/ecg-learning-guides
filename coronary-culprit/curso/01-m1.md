# 1. Leads are cameras

The injury vector and why reciprocity is arithmetic

Duración indicada: 20 min

## Objetivos

- Predict ST elevation or depression in any lead from the direction of the injury vector
- Explain reciprocal change as geometry, not a second ischaemic area
- Know which leads look at the posterior wall

## 1. The injury current points at the injured wall

Tipo: read · Identificador: m1s1

Ischaemic myocardium is partly depolarised at rest, so during the ST segment current flows from healthy to injured muscle. Add up those currents and you get one ST vector pointing toward the injured wall.
Each lead is a camera. If the vector points at a lead’s positive electrode, that lead records ST elevation; if it points away, ST depression; if it runs side-on, little change.
Most machines record leads I and II and calculate III, aVR, aVL and aVF from them (III = II − I). Reciprocal change in the limb leads is therefore arithmetic, not a second area of ischaemia.

Escenario: proxLAD

## 6. Check: vectors and leads

Tipo: check · Identificador: m1s6


### An ST vector points down and to the patient’s right, toward +120°. Which limb lead shows the most ST elevation?

1. III
2. II
3. aVF
4. aVL

**Respuesta del original:** III

**Explicación:** Lead III sits at +120°, so the vector projects fully onto it. aVL, at −30°, lies almost opposite and shows the deepest depression. This is why RCA occlusions raise III more than II.

### In inferior OMI, why is aVL depressed?

1. Its axis (−30°) is nearly opposite lead III, so the same ST vector projects negatively
2. The high lateral wall is also ischaemic
3. aVL is a right-sided lead
4. The heart rotates during infarction

**Respuesta del original:** Its axis (−30°) is nearly opposite lead III, so the same ST vector projects negatively

**Explicación:** Reciprocal depression is the same vector seen from the other side. It is why aVL depression is such a sensitive early sign of inferior OMI.

### Injury to the posterior (inferolateral) wall appears on the standard 12-lead as:

1. ST depression maximal in V1–V3 with upright T waves
2. ST elevation in V5–V6 only
3. ST elevation in aVR
4. No change at all

**Respuesta del original:** ST depression maximal in V1–V3 with upright T waves

**Explicación:** V1–V3 face the posterior wall from the front, so posterior STE appears inverted as anterior STD. V7–V9 show it directly.

### On most ECG machines, lead III is:

1. Calculated as II − I
2. A separate electrode on the left leg
3. Calculated as I − II
4. The average of aVL and aVF

**Respuesta del original:** Calculated as II − I

**Explicación:** Only I and II need to be recorded; III and the augmented leads follow from Einthoven’s law. Limb-lead reciprocity always balances for that reason.
