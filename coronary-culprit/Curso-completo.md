# 1. Leads are cameras

The injury vector and why reciprocity is arithmetic

Duración indicada: 20 min

## Objetivos

- Predict ST elevation or depression in any lead from the direction of the injury vector
- Explain reciprocal change as geometry, not a second ischaemic area
- Know which leads look at the posterior wall

## 1. The injury current points at the injured wall

Tipo: read · Identificador: m1s1

Ischaemic myocardium is partly depolarised at rest, so during the ST segment current flows from healthy to injured muscle. Add up those currents and you get one ST vector pointing toward the injured wall.
Each lead is a camera. If the vector points at a lead’s positive electrode, that lead records ST elevation; if it points away, ST depression; if it runs side-on, little change.
Most machines record leads I and II and calculate III, aVR, aVL and aVF from them (III = II − I). Reciprocal change in the limb leads is therefore arithmetic, not a second area of ischaemia.

Escenario: proxLAD

## 2. Look from V2

Tipo: task · Identificador: m1s2


Click lead V2 on the ECG to see the heart from V2’s electrode. The yellow ST vector points straight at you, so V2 is elevated.

## 3. Look from behind

Tipo: task · Identificador: m1s3


Now click a lead that shows ST depression. From that electrode the injured wall is on the far side of the heart.

## 4. Make lead III win

Tipo: task · Identificador: m1s4


Move the clot to a different artery so that lead III elevates more than lead II. Think about where +120° points.

## 5. Predict before you look

Tipo: task · Identificador: m1s5


Predict first is on. Occlude any artery (or keep this one), predict all 12 leads, then reveal. Score 10 of 12 or better.

## 6. Check: vectors and leads

Tipo: check · Identificador: m1s6


### An ST vector points down and to the patient’s right, toward +120°. Which limb lead shows the most ST elevation?

1. III
2. II
3. aVF
4. aVL

**Respuesta del original:** III

**Explicación:** Lead III sits at +120°, so the vector projects fully onto it. aVL, at −30°, lies almost opposite and shows the deepest depression. This is why RCA occlusions raise III more than II.

### In inferior OMI, why is aVL depressed?

1. Its axis (−30°) is nearly opposite lead III, so the same ST vector projects negatively
2. The high lateral wall is also ischaemic
3. aVL is a right-sided lead
4. The heart rotates during infarction

**Respuesta del original:** Its axis (−30°) is nearly opposite lead III, so the same ST vector projects negatively

**Explicación:** Reciprocal depression is the same vector seen from the other side. It is why aVL depression is such a sensitive early sign of inferior OMI.

### Injury to the posterior (inferolateral) wall appears on the standard 12-lead as:

1. ST depression maximal in V1–V3 with upright T waves
2. ST elevation in V5–V6 only
3. ST elevation in aVR
4. No change at all

**Respuesta del original:** ST depression maximal in V1–V3 with upright T waves

**Explicación:** V1–V3 face the posterior wall from the front, so posterior STE appears inverted as anterior STD. V7–V9 show it directly.

### On most ECG machines, lead III is:

1. Calculated as II − I
2. A separate electrode on the left leg
3. Calculated as I − II
4. The average of aVL and aVF

**Respuesta del original:** Calculated as II − I

**Explicación:** Only I and II need to be recorded; III and the augmented leads follow from Einthoven’s law. Limb-lead reciprocity always balances for that reason.

# 2. Territories and branch points

Which leads drop out as the clot moves

Duración indicada: 25 min

## Objetivos

- Map LAD, circumflex and RCA territories onto the 17 segments and the RV
- Use V1, aVL and V4R to locate the clot relative to S1, D1 and the RV branch
- Recognise high lateral OMI

## 1. Branch order decides the ECG

Tipo: read · Identificador: m2s1

The LAD supplies the anterior wall and septum (through septal perforators) and the anterolateral wall (through diagonals). It often wraps the apex. The circumflex supplies the lateral and inferolateral walls through obtuse marginals. The RCA supplies the RV through its marginal branch, and in most people the inferior wall and inferior septum.
A clot cuts off everything downstream. Before S1, V1 rises (septum). Before D1, I and aVL rise (high lateral wall). Before the RV branch, V1 and V4R rise (right ventricle). Move the clot past a branch and those leads fall away.

Escenario: proxLAD

## 2. Slide down the LAD

Tipo: task · Identificador: m2s2


Press the ▸ travel button on the occlusion chip and let the clot slide all the way down the LAD. Watch V1 fall after S1 and aVL after D1.

## 3. Find the RV branch

Tipo: task · Identificador: m2s3


Find an RCA position where V4R is elevated (RV involved), then move the clot so V4R is no longer elevated.

## 4. Raise the South African flag

Tipo: task · Identificador: m2s4


Without loading the preset, occlude the branch that produces STE in I, aVL and V2 with STD in III.

## 5. Territory drill

Tipo: drill · Identificador: m2s5


Answer 15 Territory items with 70% or better.

## 6. Check: territories

Tipo: check · Identificador: m2s6


### STE in V1 alongside anterior STE suggests the occlusion is:

1. Proximal to the first septal perforator
2. Distal to the second diagonal
3. In the right coronary artery
4. In the circumflex

**Respuesta del original:** Proximal to the first septal perforator

**Explicación:** S1 supplies the basal septum, which V1 faces. Losing it adds V1 elevation, so V1 STE marks a proximal LAD clot.

### STE in I, aVL and V2 with STD in III, and little change in V3–V5, points to:

1. The first diagonal (high lateral wall)
2. The distal RCA
3. A wraparound LAD
4. The left main

**Respuesta del original:** The first diagonal (high lateral wall)

**Explicación:** This is the South African flag of high lateral OMI from D1, a ramus or a high OM.

### In proximal RCA occlusion with RV infarction, V1 is typically:

1. Elevated, while V2 is depressed
2. Depressed along with V2
3. Unchanged
4. Elevated more than V3 and V4

**Respuesta del original:** Elevated, while V2 is depressed

**Explicación:** The RV vector raises V1 while the posterior component depresses V2. V4R confirms the RV involvement.

### Which territory most often produces an ECG with no diagnostic ST change?

1. Circumflex and obtuse marginals
2. Proximal LAD
3. Proximal RCA
4. Left main

**Respuesta del original:** Circumflex and obtuse marginals

**Explicación:** The lateral and inferolateral walls face away from most standard leads. Echo and posterior leads matter most here.

# 3. Anatomical variants

Dominance, wraparound LAD and the ramus

Duración indicada: 15 min

## Objetivos

- Predict how dominance changes the inferior-wall ECG
- Recognise a wraparound LAD
- Separate a dominant circumflex from the RCA

## 1. Who owns the inferior wall?

Tipo: read · Identificador: m3s1

About 70% of people are right-dominant (the RCA gives the PDA), about 20% co-dominant and about 10% left-dominant (the circumflex gives the PDA). The dominant artery also gives the AV nodal artery, so dominant-vessel occlusions cause AV block.
A "type III" LAD wraps round the apex to supply the inferior apex, so a distal LAD clot can raise II, III and aVF. A ramus intermedius, present in roughly a fifth of people, supplies the high lateral wall like D1.

Escenario: leftDomLCx

## 2. Hand the inferior wall to the circumflex

Tipo: task · Identificador: m3s2


Keep the clot in the proximal circumflex and switch Anatomy → dominance to Left. Watch the inferior leads join in.

## 3. Wrap the LAD

Tipo: task · Identificador: m3s3


Make a distal LAD occlusion raise II, III and aVF as well as the chest leads. Hint: look in Anatomy.

## 4. Occlude a ramus

Tipo: task · Identificador: m3s4


Add a ramus intermedius (Anatomy) and occlude it. Compare its ECG with a D1 occlusion.

## 5. Contrast drill: dominant circumflex vs distal RCA

Tipo: drill · Identificador: m3s5


Ten items, 80% or better. Look at II versus III and at lead I.

## 6. Check: variants

Tipo: check · Identificador: m3s6


### In a left-dominant system the posterior descending artery arises from:

1. The circumflex
2. The RCA
3. The LAD
4. The ramus

**Respuesta del original:** The circumflex

**Explicación:** Left dominance means the circumflex reaches the crux and gives the PDA (and the AV nodal artery).

### Inferior STE with II ≥ III and no STD in lead I favours:

1. A (dominant) circumflex culprit
2. The RCA
3. The LAD
4. Pericarditis

**Respuesta del original:** A (dominant) circumflex culprit

**Explicación:** The circumflex injury vector points leftward, toward II and away from III.

### A distal LAD occlusion causes anterior and inferior STE when:

1. The LAD wraps round the apex
2. The patient is left-dominant
3. A ramus is present
4. The RCA is also occluded

**Respuesta del original:** The LAD wraps round the apex

**Explicación:** The wraparound segment supplies the inferior apex.

### Roughly what proportion of people are right-dominant?

1. About 70%
2. About 10%
3. About 40%
4. Almost everyone

**Respuesta del original:** About 70%

**Explicación:** Roughly 70% right, 20% co-dominant and 10% left; quoted figures vary between series.

# 4. Subtle OMI

Occlusions that fail STEMI criteria

Duración indicada: 30 min

## Objetivos

- Recognise hyperacute T waves, posterior OMI, de Winter, Aslanger and LBBB with OMI
- Use posterior leads purposefully
- Act on OMI without waiting for STEMI criteria

## 1. OMI, not STEMI

Tipo: read · Identificador: m4s1

STEMI criteria miss a large share of acute occlusions. The occlusion is what matters, and it has many ECG faces: hyperacute T waves before the ST lifts, posterior STE seen as anterior STD, high lateral changes in non-contiguous leads, de Winter T waves, the Aslanger pattern, and OMI hidden in LBBB.
In each of these, the vector logic from Module 1 still holds. The patterns only look strange because the vector points somewhere the standard criteria don’t look.

Escenario: hyperLAD

## 2. Watch hyperacute T waves evolve

Tipo: task · Identificador: m4s2


This is minutes after LAD occlusion. Drag the timeline to Acute and watch the bulky T waves give way to ST elevation.

## 3. Confirm a posterior OMI

Tipo: task · Identificador: m4s3


The 12-lead shows only V1–V3 depression. Switch on V4R · V7–V9 and confirm the posterior STE.

## 4. Build the Aslanger pattern

Tipo: task · Identificador: m4s4


Without loading the preset, occlude an inferior branch (try partway down the PDA) and add diffuse subendocardial ischaemia in Modifiers until only lead III stays elevated.

## 5. Signs drill

Tipo: drill · Identificador: m4s5


Answer 20 Signs items with 75% or better.

## 6. Two patients

Tipo: case · Identificador: m4s6


Run both cases and score at least 70 in each.

## 7. Check: subtle OMI

Tipo: check · Identificador: m4s7


### Hyperacute T waves are best judged by:

1. Their size and bulk relative to the QRS in the same lead
2. An absolute height above 10 mm
3. Their presence in aVR
4. Whether the QT is long

**Respuesta del original:** Their size and bulk relative to the QRS in the same lead

**Explicación:** Proportion is the key: a broad, bulky T wave that dwarfs a modest QRS is suspicious whatever its absolute height.

### de Winter T waves show:

1. Upsloping J-point depression with tall, symmetric precordial T waves
2. Deep symmetric T inversion in V1–V4
3. Diffuse STE with PR depression
4. Peaked narrow T waves with a wide QRS

**Respuesta del original:** Upsloping J-point depression with tall, symmetric precordial T waves

**Explicación:** A STEMI equivalent of proximal LAD occlusion.

### In LBBB, the Smith-modified Sgarbossa rule flags OMI when:

1. Discordant STE is ≥25% of the preceding S wave
2. Any STE ≥1 mm appears in V1–V3
3. The QRS is wider than 140 ms
4. The T waves are discordant

**Respuesta del original:** Discordant STE is ≥25% of the preceding S wave

**Explicación:** Concordant STE or STD in V1–V3 also count; excessive discordance is the commonest positive.

### The Aslanger pattern is STE in lead III only plus:

1. STD in V4–V6 but not V2, with V1 higher than V2
2. STE in aVR and V1 only
3. Posterior STE in V7–V9
4. Q waves in V1–V3

**Respuesta del original:** STD in V4–V6 but not V2, with V1 higher than V2

**Explicación:** An inferior OMI vector plus a subendocardial vector toward aVR.

# 5. Time course and reperfusion

Hyperacute, acute, evolving, reperfused, re-occluded

Duración indicada: 20 min

## Objetivos

- Date an occlusion from the ECG
- Recognise reperfusion T waves, including posterior
- Spot pseudo-normalisation from re-occlusion

## 1. The ECG is a clock

Tipo: read · Identificador: m5s1

Occlusion brings hyperacute T waves within minutes, then ST elevation, then over hours Q waves (loss of R) and T inversion as the ST settles.
Reperfusion is different: the ST resolves quickly and the T waves invert in the leads that were elevated (Wellens in the LAD). Seen from the front, posterior reperfusion gives tall T waves in V1–V3. If reperfusion T waves turn upright again while pain returns, the artery has re-occluded. The echo stays abnormal for days because reperfused muscle is stunned.

Escenario: wellensB

## 2. Run the clock

Tipo: task · Identificador: m5s2


Press ▶ on the timeline and let the occlusion run to Completed. Watch the Q waves form and the T waves invert.

## 3. Make Wellens

Tipo: task · Identificador: m5s3


Reperfuse the artery (Artery → Reperfused · late) and find the Wellens T waves.

## 4. Posterior reperfusion

Tipo: task · Identificador: m5s4


Reperfuse this circumflex occlusion (late) and find the tall T waves in V1–V3.

## 5. Two patients

Tipo: case · Identificador: m5s5


Run both cases and score at least 70 in each.

## 6. Check: time course

Tipo: check · Identificador: m5s6


### Wellens T waves mean the LAD:

1. Occluded and reperfused, leaving a critical lesion that can re-occlude
2. Is occluded right now
3. Was never involved
4. Is filled by collaterals

**Respuesta del original:** Occluded and reperfused, leaving a critical lesion that can re-occlude

**Explicación:** Urgent angiography; stress testing can provoke re-occlusion.

### Tall upright T waves in V1–V3 after a circumflex occlusion settles suggest:

1. Posterior reperfusion
2. A new anterior OMI
3. Hyperkalaemia
4. LVH

**Respuesta del original:** Posterior reperfusion

**Explicación:** Posterior T inversion seen from the front.

### Wellens T waves become upright again as pain returns. This is:

1. Pseudo-normalisation from re-occlusion
2. Healing
3. Reperfusion
4. A benign variant

**Respuesta del original:** Pseudo-normalisation from re-occlusion

**Explicación:** Treat it as an occlusion: the next ECG may show STE.

### After successful reperfusion, regional wall motion:

1. Can stay abnormal for days (stunning) before recovering
2. Normalises immediately
3. Becomes hyperkinetic
4. Is unaffected because the artery is open

**Respuesta del original:** Can stay abnormal for days (stunning) before recovering

**Explicación:** So an echo after reperfusion still shows the territory.

# 6. Mimics and the echo

ST elevation without occlusion, and the echo as tiebreaker

Duración indicada: 30 min

## Objetivos

- Separate pericarditis, early repolarisation, LVH, hyperkalaemia, aneurysm and Takotsubo from OMI
- Use regional wall motion to support or refute OMI
- Know when angiography is still needed

## 1. What each mimic lacks

Tipo: read · Identificador: m6s1

Pericarditis has diffuse STE without reciprocal STD except in aVR and V1, and never depresses aVL. Early repolarisation has proportionate T waves and preserved S waves. LVH STE is proportional to the S wave. Hyperkalaemic T waves are narrow-based with a wide QRS. An aneurysm has small T waves relative to the QRS. Takotsubo crosses territories.
Echo is the tiebreaker. OMI gives a regional wall motion abnormality in one coronary distribution within seconds. Pericarditis, early repolarisation and hyperkalaemia do not. Takotsubo gives one that no single artery could cause, and still needs angiography.

Escenario: pericarditis

## 2. aVL: pericarditis vs inferior OMI

Tipo: task · Identificador: m6s2


Note aVL in pericarditis. Then click the RCA to occlude it and compare aVL.

## 3. Takotsubo on echo

Tipo: task · Identificador: m6s3


Switch the echo highlight on and open the PSAX view. The abnormality runs all the way round, which no single artery can do.

## 4. Mimics drill

Tipo: drill · Identificador: m6s4


Answer 20 Mimics items with 75% or better.

## 5. Echo drill

Tipo: drill · Identificador: m6s5


Answer 10 Echo items with 70% or better.

## 6. Three patients

Tipo: case · Identificador: m6s6


Run all three cases and score at least 70 in each.

## 7. Check: mimics

Tipo: check · Identificador: m6s7


### The single most useful ECG feature separating inferior OMI from pericarditis is:

1. STD in aVL (present in OMI, absent in pericarditis)
2. PR depression
3. Tachycardia
4. STE in V5–V6

**Respuesta del original:** STD in aVL (present in OMI, absent in pericarditis)

**Explicación:** PR depression supports pericarditis but also occurs in OMI with atrial involvement; aVL is the stronger discriminator.

### An old LV aneurysm rather than acute OMI is suggested when, in V1–V4:

1. Every T/QRS amplitude ratio is below 0.36
2. The T waves are bulky
3. There is STE in aVR
4. The QRS is narrow

**Respuesta del original:** Every T/QRS amplitude ratio is below 0.36

**Explicación:** A ratio above 0.36 in any of V1–V4 suggests new occlusion on top of old infarction.

### Takotsubo’s echo is distinctive because:

1. The wall motion abnormality crosses coronary territories, with a hyperkinetic base
2. The apex is hyperkinetic
3. There is always a large effusion
4. Wall motion is normal

**Respuesta del original:** The wall motion abnormality crosses coronary territories, with a hyperkinetic base

**Explicación:** Even so, the ECG cannot exclude LAD occlusion, so angiography is needed.

### Hyperkalaemic T waves differ from hyperacute T waves because they are:

1. Narrow-based and peaked, with a wide QRS and flat P waves
2. Broad-based and bulky
3. Confined to one coronary territory
4. Inverted

**Respuesta del original:** Narrow-based and peaked, with a wide QRS and flat P waves

**Explicación:** Hyperacute T waves are broad, bulky and territorial with a normal QRS.
