## One patient at a time, on a real clock.

Each case starts with an arrival ECG. Repeat it, add right-sided and posterior leads, scan with bedside echo or send a troponin: every action moves the clock on, and the patient’s heart keeps evolving underneath. Some arteries occlude after arrival, some reperfuse and re-occlude, and some ECGs are mimics.

You are scored on how soon you activate after the first real opportunity, and you lose everything for a missed OMI or a false activation. Pick a case to begin.

About the model

**What it simulates.** The left ventricle is divided into the 17 AHA segments plus two RV regions. Each artery segment owns a share of each region's blood supply, which shifts with dominance, a wraparound LAD and a ramus. Occlude a point and everything downstream becomes ischaemic in proportion to the supply it loses.

**How the ECG is made.** Each ischaemic region contributes an injury current. In the limb leads it acts as a frontal-plane vector: only leads I and II are synthesised and the other four are derived (III = II − I, and so on), so reciprocal change is exact. The chest leads combine projection with proximity, which is why the posterior wall shows up as STD in V1–V3 and the high lateral wall reaches V2.

**Time course.** Hyperacute T waves come first, then ST elevation with terminal QRS distortion, then Q waves and T inversion. Reperfusion resolves the ST segments and produces reperfusion T waves (Wellens in the LAD, tall T waves in V1–V3 for the posterior wall).

**Echo.** Each segment's wall motion is set by the share of its blood supply it has lost: hypokinetic, then akinetic, and dyskinetic with wall thinning once an apical infarct is complete. Remote segments become hyperkinetic, and reperfused muscle stays stunned. The four views show the standard segments of the 17-segment model, and the LVEF and wall motion score index are rough estimates from those grades.

**Limits.** This is a teaching model with idealised geometry and amplitudes. Real ECGs vary with body habitus, lead placement, collaterals, prior infarction, LVH and timing. Use it to build intuition about vectors and territories, not to calibrate millimetre thresholds. ST values are measured at J + 40 ms.
