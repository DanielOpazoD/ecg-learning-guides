## Aortic stenosis

LV pressure load

In calcific aortic stenosis, the valve orifice narrows, the transvalvular jet velocity increases and the LV wall thickens in response to the pressure load. In the late stage, LV systolic dysfunction reduces the stroke volume, and the transvalvular gradient decreases with it.

### Natural history

1. In **progressive stenosis**, mild stenosis has a peak velocity of 2.0–2.9 m/s or a mean gradient below 20 mmHg, and moderate stenosis has a peak velocity of 3.0–3.9 m/s or a mean gradient of 20–39 mmHg. Early LV diastolic dysfunction can be present, and the LVEF is normal[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
2. **Severe stenosis** is defined by a peak velocity ≥4 m/s or a mean gradient ≥40 mmHg, and the valve area is typically ≤1.0 cm². The LV has diastolic dysfunction and mild hypertrophy, with a normal LVEF[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38). Very severe stenosis is defined by a peak velocity ≥5 m/s or a mean gradient ≥60 mmHg[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
3. **LV dysfunction** in severe stenosis is defined by an LVEF below 50%[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
4. In **low-flow, low-gradient stenosis**, LV systolic dysfunction reduces the transvalvular flow; the area is ≤1.0 cm², with a peak velocity below 4 m/s or a mean gradient below 40 mmHg[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).

The ACC/AHA stages also require the symptom status: patients in stage C have no symptoms, and patients in stage D have symptoms[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38). The figure and the diagram model the echocardiographic findings only, and the diagram names the echocardiographic pattern, not the stage.

### Grading

AVA = LVOT area × VTI(LVOT) ÷ VTI(AV)  ·  ΔP = 4v²  ·  mean gradient = mean of 4v² over ejection

Chapter [07 Doppler hemodynamics](https://intro2echo.com/pressure) describes the stroke volume from the LVOT and the simplified Bernoulli equation. The stroke volume that crosses the LVOT also crosses the valve. The valve area therefore equals the LVOT area × the LVOT VTI ÷ the AV VTI, the continuity equation[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). The diagram grades each criterion by Table 3 of the EACVI/ASE recommendations[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). Table 3 grades a peak velocity of 2.5 m/s or less as aortic sclerosis without stenosis[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). The ACC/AHA progressive stage starts at 2.0 m/s[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).

- The highest jet velocity should be recorded with continuous-wave (CW) Doppler from several acoustic windows, and the window that gives it should be reported; angle correction should not be used[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30) ( [chapter 03](https://intro2echo.com/shift)).
- The peak gradient is 4 × (peak velocity)². The mean gradient is the average of the instantaneous gradients over ejection, from the traced CW envelope[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30).
- A peak velocity ≥4.0 m/s, a mean gradient ≥40 mmHg or an area <1.0 cm² each suggests severe stenosis; ideally, all three criteria are concordant[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). A velocity ratio below 0.25 also indicates severe stenosis[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30).
- The dimensionless index is the ratio of the LVOT VTI to the AV VTI. The velocity ratio is the ratio of the LVOT peak velocity to the AV peak velocity. Neither uses the LVOT diameter. The diagram grades both by the velocity-ratio cut-offs of Table 3.
- As the obstruction becomes more severe, the velocity peaks later in systole and the Doppler envelope becomes more rounded; in mild stenosis, the velocity peaks early and the envelope is triangular[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30).

In the diagram above, a change in one measurement moves the slider to a band of the new result. A move of the slider returns every value to the model. The slider has no band for paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient stenosis: in the model, the LVEF falls before the gradient does.

### Low-flow, low-gradient aortic stenosis

A high gradient is a peak velocity ≥4 m/s or a mean gradient ≥40 mmHg. It indicates severe stenosis whether the flow and the LV function are normal or reduced[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). With an area <1.0 cm² and a low gradient, the flow and the LVEF name the pattern[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). Low flow is defined as a stroke volume index below 35 mL/m²[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30).

- **Classical low-flow, low-gradient stenosis** has an LVEF <50%[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). During low-dose dobutamine echocardiography, a velocity ≥4.0 m/s with an area that remains ≤1.0 cm² confirms severe stenosis, whereas an increase in area to above 1.0 cm² suggests pseudo-severe stenosis[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). Stage D2 also requires symptoms and severe stenosis confirmed by dobutamine echocardiography[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
- In **paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient stenosis**, the LVEF is ≥50%. A small LV with a low stroke volume produces a low gradient across a severe stenosis[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). Stage D3 also requires symptoms[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
- **Normal-flow, low-gradient stenosis** has an LVEF ≥50% and a stroke volume index ≥35 mL/m²[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). It often reflects an error of measurement, most often an LVOT diameter that is measured too small[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30). At normal flow, a mean gradient of 40 mmHg corresponds to an area nearer 0.8 than 1.0 cm²[30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30).

### Key points

1. The thresholds for severe aortic stenosis are a peak velocity of 4 m/s, a mean gradient of 40 mmHg and a valve area of 1.0 cm²; ideally, all three criteria are concordant.
2. The continuity equation squares an error in the LVOT diameter. The dimensionless index and the velocity ratio do not use the diameter.
3. LV wall thickening develops first, and the LVEF falls only late in the course of the disease.
4. A low gradient with an area below 1.0 cm² needs an assessment of the flow and the LVEF.

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. A patient has a peak aortic velocity of 3.6 m/s, a mean gradient of 30 mmHg, an AVA of 0.8 cm², a stroke volume index of 28 mL/m² and an LVEF of 60%. What is the most likely diagnosis?

1. Moderate aortic stenosis
2. Paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient severe stenosis
3. Doppler measurement error

**Respuesta del original:** Paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient severe stenosis

**Explicación:** A small valve area with a low gradient, low flow and a normal LVEF is consistent with paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient severe stenosis.

### 2. A patient has an LVEF of 35%, a peak aortic velocity of 3.4 m/s and an AVA of 0.8 cm². During low-dose dobutamine echocardiography, the area increases to 1.3 cm² and the velocity changes little. What is the most likely diagnosis?

1. Pseudo-severe stenosis
2. True severe stenosis
3. Very severe stenosis

**Respuesta del original:** Pseudo-severe stenosis

**Explicación:** An increase in area to above 1.0 cm² with increased flow suggests pseudo-severe stenosis; in true severe stenosis, the area remains ≤1.0 cm² and the velocity reaches 4 m/s.

### 3. In severe aortic stenosis without symptoms, which finding defines ACC/AHA stage C2?

1. LVEF below 50%
2. Peak velocity 5 m/s
3. Septal thickness 1.3 cm

**Respuesta del original:** LVEF below 50%

**Explicación:** Stage C2 is severe stenosis with LV systolic dysfunction, defined as an LVEF below 50%. A peak velocity of 5 m/s indicates very severe stenosis, which remains stage C1.
