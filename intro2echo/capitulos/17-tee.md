### The probe and its movements

The probe tip is advanced into the esophagus or the stomach, or withdrawn[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Rotating the probe clockwise, toward the patient's right, is turning to the right; rotating it counterclockwise is turning to the left[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). The large control wheel flexes the tip anteriorly (anteflexion) or posteriorly (retroflexion), and the small wheel to the patient's right or left[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Buttons on the handle rotate the imaging plane electronically from 0° to 180°: toward 180° is forward rotation, toward 0° backward rotation[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). The basic examination uses three positions: the upper esophageal (UE), the mid-esophageal (ME) and the transgastric (TG) level[186](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-186). The transthoracic views are in [chapter 04](https://intro2echo.com/windows).

### Image orientation

The transducer is at the top of the image, with the near field close to it[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). At 0°, the transverse plane, the patient's right appears on the left of the display[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Forward rotation to 90°, the longitudinal plane, moves the left of the display inferiorly[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). At 180°, the image is the mirror image of 0°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). From the mid-esophagus, the LA lies in the near field. An icon on the screen shows the angle[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).

### The standard views

The ASE/SCA guideline describes 28 views of a comprehensive examination, each with its level, approximate angle, probe movement from the previous view and the structures imaged[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Flexion or turning may be needed in a given patient[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). The basic perioperative examination uses 11 of the views and starts in the ME four-chamber view[186](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-186). The figure shows 17 views, including all 11 basic views.

- **Mid-esophageal, neutral.** From the four-chamber view at 0–10°, forward rotation gives the mitral commissural view at 50–70°, the two-chamber view at 80–100° and the long-axis view at 120–140°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). The long-axis view has the orientation of the transthoracic long-axis or three-chamber view[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
- **Mid-esophageal, turned right.** In the aortic valve short-axis view at 25–45°, the left coronary cusp lies in the near field on the right of the display, the noncoronary cusp next to the atrial septum and the right coronary cusp in the far field[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). The RV inflow-outflow view lies at 50–70°, the bicaval view at 90–110° and the aortic valve long-axis view at 120–140°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
- **Mid-esophageal, turned left.** From the two-chamber view at 90–110°, turning to the left gives the LA appendage view[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Advancing or anteflexing the probe may open the appendage, and the left upper pulmonary vein often shows in the same view[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
- **Upper esophageal.** The ascending aorta appears in long axis at 90–110°, with the right pulmonary artery behind it, and in short axis at 0–30°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Table 10 of the guideline gives these two ME views the upper esophageal level[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). Higher, the aortic arch appears in long axis at 0–10° and in short axis at 70–90°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
- **Transgastric.** The anteflexed probe shows the LV in short axis at the papillary muscles at 0–20°, with myocardium of the left anterior descending, circumflex and right coronary arteries together[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). At 90–110°, the TG two-chamber view shows the anterior and inferior walls[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
- **Descending aorta.** Turned to the left, the probe shows the descending aorta in short axis at 0–10° and in long axis at 90–100°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). It lies posterior to the esophagus and to its left[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).

### What TEE adds to transthoracic imaging

TEE is indicated when its findings will change management and TTE is nondiagnostic, or likely to be[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). TTE may be nondiagnostic for far-field structures, such as the aorta and the LA appendage, and for prosthetic valves, native valve masses and paravalvular abscesses[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). The guideline rates TEE as appropriate in suspected acute aortic pathology, including dissection, and in suspected endocarditis with a moderate or high pretest probability[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). [Chapter 21](https://intro2echo.com/aorta) covers aortic dissection, [bonus chapter B6](https://intro2echo.com/prosthetic) prosthetic valves and [bonus chapter B7](https://intro2echo.com/endocarditis) endocarditis.

The LA appendage is the primary target in the search for a cardiac source of embolus[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). TEE detects appendage thrombus with a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 99%, but thrombi smaller than 2 mm can be missed[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). In the ACUTE trial, in atrial fibrillation of more than two days, early cardioversion guided by TEE had an embolic rate similar to that of cardioversion after 3 weeks of warfarin, with fewer hemorrhagic events[187](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-187).

In an agitated saline study, more than three bubbles in the left heart within three beats of right heart opacification are most consistent with a patent foramen ovale[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90). [Bonus chapter B8](https://intro2echo.com/shunts) shows the shunts.

### The interactive figure

The left pane shows the heart model from the patient's left, with the esophagus behind the LA, the probe at its level and its imaging plane. The right pane shows the echo image that the plane cuts from the same mesh, in the guideline's orientation. The Level and Turn switches move the probe, and the slider rotates the plane from 0° to 180°; when it stops near the angle of a view, it snaps to that view. Between two views of one level and turn, the probe moves from one view's position to the other's.

The Free probe switch releases the probe. Four sliders then advance or withdraw it along the esophagus, turn it, ante- or retroflex it and flex it to the side. With the probe free, the multiplane angle turns the plane about the beam, and the probe does not move. The panel names the nearest standard view, with the difference in degrees and centimeters. Play shows the views in the guideline's order. After the four-chamber view, it frees the probe and moves each control out and back, then all of them together.

The esophagus runs behind the LA of each heart and to the right of the descending aorta, then through the hiatus to the stomach under the LV. In this model, the probe is retroflexed and flexed to the right to show the four-chamber plane at 5°. The mesh's descending aorta ends a little above the hiatus, so for the aortic views the probe lies behind the LA, where the aorta crosses the whole sector.

### Key points

1. The probe is advanced or withdrawn, turned right or left, and flexed; the imaging plane rotates electronically from 0° to 180°[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
2. The transducer is at the top of the image. At 0° the patient's right is on the left of the display, and 180° gives the mirror image[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
3. The basic perioperative examination uses 11 views and starts in the ME four-chamber view[186](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-186).
4. TEE shows structures that TTE may not: the LA appendage, the aorta, prosthetic valves and abscesses[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
