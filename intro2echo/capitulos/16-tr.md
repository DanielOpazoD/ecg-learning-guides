## Tricuspid regurgitation

Right heart volume overload

Tricuspid regurgitation imposes a volume overload on the right heart. The RV, the tricuspid annulus and the RA dilate, and the systemic veins distend. In torrential regurgitation, the jet velocity falls because the RV and RA pressures approach each other.

### Echocardiographic findings by grade

1. In **mild** regurgitation, the jet is small, narrow and central, the CW Doppler signal is faint, hepatic vein flow is systolic-dominant and the IVC diameter is below 2 cm[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29); the RA pressure estimate uses 2.1 cm ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)).
2. In **moderate** regurgitation, the RV and RA are normal in size or mildly dilated, hepatic vein flow shows systolic blunting, the vena contracta is 0.3–0.69 cm and the EROA is 0.20–0.39 cm²[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29).
3. In **severe** regurgitation, the vena contracta is ≥0.7 cm, the EROA ≥0.40 cm², the regurgitant volume ≥45 mL, the PISA radius >0.9 cm (with the color baseline shifted to an aliasing velocity of 28 cm/s) and the jet area >10 cm²; hepatic vein flow shows systolic reversal, the tricuspid E velocity is >1.0 m/s and the IVC diameter is >2.5 cm[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). The ACC/AHA criteria add a dilated RV and RA and an elevated RA pressure with a c-V wave[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
4. Two grades beyond severe are defined: **massive** regurgitation, with a vena contracta of 1.4–2.0 cm, an EROA of 0.60–0.79 cm² and a regurgitant volume of 60–74 mL, and **torrential** regurgitation, with a vena contracta ≥2.1 cm, an EROA ≥0.80 cm² and a regurgitant volume ≥75 mL[65](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-65). An echocardiography report may describe massive and torrential TR as subgroups of severe TR[36](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-36).

### Integrated grading

The ASE algorithm first asks whether most of the specific criteria for mild or for severe TR are present[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). The guideline text asks for a majority of the specific signs, so the diagram counts four of the seven. If neither set has a majority, the TR is probably moderate, and the vena contracta, the EROA and the regurgitant volume decide between mild, moderate and severe[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). Unlike MR and AR, TR has no validated quantitative grades I to IV, and clinical experience with its quantitation is limited[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). If the findings are inconsistent, the grade is indeterminate, and TEE or CMR can quantify the regurgitation[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). The algorithm gives no rule for discordant quantitative measures; the diagram uses the middle grade of the three. It uses the same rule for the EACVI grades beyond severe[65](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-65).

### Pitfalls

- The peak TR velocity does not indicate the regurgitant volume. Very severe TR often has a low peak velocity, about 2 m/s, because the RV and RA pressures equalize[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29); the EACVI lists a peak velocity below 2 m/s for massive TR[65](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-65). A triangular jet signal then underestimates the RV systolic pressure[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32) ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)).
- Systolic flow reversal in the hepatic veins is specific for severe TR once other causes (atrial fibrillation, pacing, heart block, respiration, preload, altered RA or RV compliance) have been excluded[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29).
- The regurgitant volume threshold for severe TR (45 mL) is lower than that for severe MR (60 mL) at the same EROA, because the TR velocity is lower[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). The regurgitant volume is the EROA × the VTI of the TR jet, and a slow jet has a short VTI. At a peak velocity of 2.2 m/s, an EROA of 0.80 cm² gives about 40 mL; in the figure, the vena contracta and the EROA reach the grades for massive and torrential TR before the regurgitant volume does[65](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-65).
- The figure shows the flow convergence at an aliasing velocity of 28 cm/s, and the jet as a share of the RA area at a scale of 50 to 70 cm/s, at which the jet criteria are defined[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). By the PISA method ( [chapter 15](https://intro2echo.com/mr)), the flow rate 2πr² × 28 cm/s equals the EROA × the peak TR velocity. Beyond severe TR the velocity falls as the regurgitation increases, so the PISA radius stays at 1.0–1.1 cm while the EROA rises from 0.60 to 1.21 cm².
- At the time of left-sided valve surgery, tricuspid repair is reasonable when the tricuspid annulus measures more than 4.0 cm in diastole, even with less than severe TR[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).

### Key points

1. Four or more of the seven specific criteria for mild or for severe TR define the grade; otherwise, the vena contracta, the EROA and the regurgitant volume decide.
2. Tricuspid regurgitation is severe when the vena contracta is ≥0.7 cm, the EROA is ≥0.40 cm² and the regurgitant volume is ≥45 mL.
3. Systolic flow reversal in the hepatic veins is specific for severe tricuspid regurgitation.
4. A low peak TR velocity does not exclude severe tricuspid regurgitation.

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. Why is the regurgitant volume threshold for severe regurgitation lower for TR (45 mL) than for MR (60 mL) at the same EROA?

1. The TR velocity is lower
2. The RV is smaller
3. TR jets are always eccentric

**Respuesta del original:** The TR velocity is lower

**Explicación:** The regurgitant volume equals EROA × VTI, and the lower TR velocity produces a smaller VTI.

### 2. In torrential TR, the peak jet velocity is 1.8 m/s. What explains the low velocity?

1. The RV and RA pressures approach each other
2. The regurgitation is mild
3. The Doppler beam is not aligned with the jet

**Respuesta del original:** The RV and RA pressures approach each other

**Explicación:** With a very large regurgitant orifice, the RA pressure rises toward the RV pressure, so the systolic pressure difference and the jet velocity decrease.

### 3. Which hepatic vein flow pattern is specific for severe TR?

1. Systolic flow reversal
2. Systolic dominance
3. A large a-wave

**Respuesta del original:** Systolic flow reversal

**Explicación:** Systolic flow reversal in the hepatic veins is specific for severe TR once other causes have been excluded.
