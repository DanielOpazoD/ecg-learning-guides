## Infective endocarditis

Vegetations, leaflet perforation and the echocardiographic criteria

Echocardiography shows the lesions of infective endocarditis: the vegetation, and the destruction that it causes, such as leaflet perforation and abscess. The size and mobility of a vegetation predict embolism, and a length of 10 mm is the threshold in the ESC recommendations for surgery.

**Vegetation of 10 mm or more.** A 14-mm mass is attached to the atrial side of the anterior mitral leaflet, near its line of closure, with the gray scale of myocardium[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44). It is pedunculated and prolapses across the coaptation plane, into the LA in systole and into the LV in diastole: a highly mobile vegetation[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44). Its maximal length is 10 mm or more: with another indication for surgery, urgent surgery is recommended (ESC class I), and without severe valve dysfunction or clinical embolism it may be considered at low surgical risk (class IIb)[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

### The echocardiographic criteria

In the 2023 Duke-ISCVID criteria, echocardiography or cardiac CT that shows a vegetation, a valvular or leaflet perforation, a valvular or leaflet aneurysm, an abscess, a pseudoaneurysm or an intracardiac fistula is a major criterion[73](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-73). Significant new valvular regurgitation on echocardiography and a new partial dehiscence of a prosthetic valve, compared with previous imaging, are major criteria too; worsening or a change of preexisting regurgitation is not sufficient[73](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-73). The 2023 ESC criteria count the lesions characteristic of IE on echocardiography, cardiac CT, \[18F\]FDG-PET/CT or WBC SPECT/CT as one major imaging criterion[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

The ESC defined the lesions on echocardiography as follows[75](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-75):

- **Vegetation.** An oscillating or non-oscillating intracardiac mass on a valve or another endocardial structure, or on implanted intracardiac material.
- **Perforation.** An interruption of endocardial tissue continuity traversed by color Doppler flow.
- **Abscess.** A thickened, non-homogeneous perivalvular area with an echodense or echolucent appearance.
- **Pseudoaneurysm.** A pulsatile perivalvular echo-free space with flow on color Doppler.
- **Fistula.** Color Doppler communication between two neighboring cavities through a perforation.
- **Valve aneurysm.** A saccular bulging of valvular tissue.

### TTE and TOE

TTE is recommended as the first-line imaging modality in suspected IE[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). TOE is recommended when TTE is negative or nondiagnostic and the clinical suspicion of IE remains, in suspected IE with a prosthetic valve or an intracardiac device, and after a positive TTE, except in isolated right-sided native valve IE with a TTE of good quality and unequivocal findings[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). When the first examination is negative or inconclusive and the suspicion remains high, repeating TTE or TOE within 5–7 days is recommended[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

TTE has limits for perivalvular complications, small vegetations, prosthetic valves and device leads[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). For leaflet perforation, the estimated sensitivity is 45% for TTE and 95% for TOE, with a specificity of 98% for both[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44).

### The vegetation

A vegetation lies typically on the atrial side of an atrioventricular valve or on the ventricular side of the aortic valve[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44). It is usually amorphous, shaggy or lobulated, with the echo texture of myocardium; a healed vegetation is brighter and often calcified[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44). It moves with a high-frequency flutter, independent of the motion of the valve[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44). The size of a vegetation is its maximal length[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

### Size, mobility and embolism

Embolic events affect 20–50% of patients with IE, and they may be clinically silent in up to 50% of patients[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). The risk is highest in the days around the diagnosis and falls within the first 2 weeks of antibiotic therapy[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). The size and mobility of the vegetation are the most important independent predictors of a new embolic event; a location on the mitral valve, a change of size under antibiotic therapy and _S. aureus_ also raise the risk[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). The greatest risk appears to be with vegetations of 10 mm or more on the anterior mitral leaflet[76](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-76), and the risk of neurological complications is particularly high with vegetations longer than 30 mm[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

In the studies of embolic risk, a highly mobile vegetation was pedunculated and prolapsed across the coaptation plane: a mitral vegetation prolapses into the LV in diastole and into the LA in systole[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44).

### Surgery to prevent embolism

The ESC recommendations use a vegetation length of 10 mm[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74):

- Urgent surgery is recommended in aortic or mitral IE with a persistent vegetation of 10 mm or more after one or more embolic episodes despite appropriate antibiotic therapy (class I).
- Urgent surgery is recommended in IE with a vegetation of 10 mm or more and another indication for surgery (class I).
- Urgent surgery may be considered in aortic or mitral IE with a vegetation of 10 mm or more, without severe valve dysfunction or clinical evidence of embolism, at low surgical risk (class IIb)[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

Urgent surgery is surgery within 3–5 days[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). The AHA statement uses mobile vegetations longer than 10 mm: in native valve IE, early surgery is reasonable with such a vegetation and severe valve regurgitation (class IIa)[76](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-76).

### Perforation and acute regurgitation

Leaflet perforation, leaflet rupture and mitral chordal rupture cause new severe regurgitation, or worsen regurgitation already present, with acute heart failure[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). Urgent surgery is recommended in aortic or mitral IE with severe acute regurgitation that causes symptoms of heart failure or echocardiographic signs of poor hemodynamic tolerance (class I), and emergency surgery, within 24 h, when it causes refractory pulmonary edema or cardiogenic shock (class I)[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).

In acute MR, the low blood pressure and the high LA pressure lower the driving pressure, so the jet is slower; it is usually markedly eccentric, so color Doppler can underestimate the MR[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). Systolic flow reversal in the pulmonary veins is usually present[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29) ( [bonus chapter B10](https://intro2echo.com/mi)). The guideline's Doppler methods for the severity of MR apply to chronic, not to acute severe MR, and TOE may identify acute severe MR better[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). The figure grades the perforation jet by its vena contracta with the cut-offs of chronic MR: below 0.3 cm mild, and 0.7 cm or more severe[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29) ( [chapter 15](https://intro2echo.com/mr)).

### Differential diagnosis

Echocardiography should distinguish the vegetation of IE from a non-bacterial thrombotic vegetation and benign intracardiac masses[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). Lambl excrescences, which were not related to systemic embolism in a TEE study, and the papillary fibroelastoma are described in [bonus chapter B9](https://intro2echo.com/masses); on a valve, a fibroelastoma lies typically on the aortic side of the aortic valve or on the ventricular side of the mitral valve, the opposite of the typical location of IE[44](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-44). Libman–Sacks vegetations, the non-bacterial thrombotic vegetations of systemic lupus erythematosus and the antiphospholipid syndrome, are rarely associated with valve dysfunction and never with valve perforation[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74). Previous scarring and severe myxomatous change can be indistinguishable from active lesions[76](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-76).

### Key points

1. A vegetation, perforation, aneurysm, abscess, pseudoaneurysm or fistula on echocardiography or CT is a major Duke-ISCVID criterion[73](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-73).
2. TOE is recommended when TTE is negative or nondiagnostic and suspicion remains, with a prosthetic valve or a device, and after a positive TTE, except in isolated right-sided native valve IE with a clear TTE[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).
3. Size, the maximal length, and mobility best predict embolism; 10 mm is the length in the ESC recommendations for urgent surgery[74](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-74).
4. In acute MR from a perforation, color Doppler can underestimate the eccentric jet; systolic flow reversal in the pulmonary veins supports severe MR[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29).
