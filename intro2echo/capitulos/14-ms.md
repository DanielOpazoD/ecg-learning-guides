## Mitral stenosis

LV inflow obstruction

### Echocardiographic findings by stage

1. In **progressive stenosis**, the commissures are fused and the leaflets dome in diastole; the valve area is >1.5 cm², the pressure half-time is <150 ms, LA enlargement is mild to moderate and pulmonary artery pressure is normal at rest[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).
2. In **severe stenosis**, the valve area is ≤1.5 cm² and the pressure half-time is ≥150 ms; LA enlargement is severe, and the PA systolic pressure is above 50 mmHg[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38).

The ASE divides progressive stenosis into mild stenosis (valve area >2.5 cm², pressure half-time <100 ms, mean gradient <5 mmHg, PA systolic pressure <30 mmHg) and moderate stenosis (valve area 2.5–1.6 cm², pressure half-time 100–149 ms, mean gradient 5–9 mmHg, PA systolic pressure 30–49 mmHg)[66](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-66).

### Grading

The ASE grades mitral stenosis with several measures: the valve area, the pressure half-time, the mean gradient and the PA systolic pressure. Agreement among them makes the grade more reliable, and when they disagree, stress echocardiography can assess the hemodynamic severity[66](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-66). Planimetry is the preferred method for the valve area[66](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-66), so the diagram grades by the planimetered area and shows whether each other measure lies in the same band.

The PA systolic pressure is estimated as the TR gradient plus the RA pressure ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)); the figure sets the RA pressure at 3 mmHg, and at 8 mmHg in severe stenosis, and in a patient the IVC gives it ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)).

### Measurement of valve area and gradient

MVA = 220 ÷ pressure half-time

- **Planimetry** of the mitral orifice is performed in the parasternal short-axis view, which also displays the commissural fusion[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38). When the methods give discordant areas, planimetry is the reference method[67](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-67).
- The **pressure half-time** is the time required for the peak transmitral pressure gradient to fall by half; at that time, the velocity has fallen to about 70% of its peak[66](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-66), [9](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-9). The constant 220 is empirical[67](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-67); Hatle described the method in 1979[68](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-68). By this equation, a pressure half-time of 100 ms corresponds to an area of 2.2 cm² and 150 ms to 1.47 cm², so near these values the area and the half-time can lie in different bands. The pressure half-time is not reliable immediately after balloon valvotomy or in the presence of severe aortic regurgitation or abnormal LV compliance[67](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-67).
- The **mean gradient**, the average of the instantaneous gradients ( [chapter 12](https://intro2echo.com/as)), varies with heart rate and transmitral flow, so it is not a criterion of severity; it should be reported together with the heart rate[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38). In the figure, the stroke volume falls as the heart rate rises, and the cardiac output stays at about 4.9 L/min. At 110 beats per minute, the diastolic filling period is shorter, and the mean gradient across a severe stenosis about doubles. At 70 beats per minute, an area of 1.5 cm² gives a mean gradient of 6.6 mmHg, in the moderate band.
- During **exercise**, the stroke volume remains unchanged, and the cardiac output increases with the heart rate alone[69](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-69). In 10 patients, supine exercise increased the heart rate from 74 to 110 beats per minute and the mean gradient from 9 to 18 mmHg[70](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-70). The pressure half-time decreased from 225 to 190 ms, so the area by 220 ÷ PHT increased from 1.0 to 1.2 cm²; at high heart rates and flows the pressure half-time is less reliable[70](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-70).

### Key points

1. Mitral stenosis is severe when the valve area is ≤1.5 cm² and the pressure half-time is ≥150 ms.
2. Planimetry is the preferred method for the valve area; the pressure half-time, the mean gradient and the PA systolic pressure support the grade.
3. The hemodynamic consequences of mitral stenosis are LA enlargement and a rise in pulmonary artery pressure.
4. The mean gradient should be reported together with the heart rate at which it is measured.

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. The mitral pressure half-time is 200 ms. What is the mitral valve area by the pressure half-time method?

1. 0.9 cm²
2. 1.1 cm²
3. 2.2 cm²

**Respuesta del original:** 1.1 cm²

**Explicación:** By the pressure half-time method, the mitral valve area is 220 ÷ 200 = 1.1 cm².

### 2. In mitral stenosis, the heart rate increases from 70 to 110 beats per minute. How does the mean transmitral gradient change?

1. It increases
2. It decreases
3. It does not change

**Respuesta del original:** It increases

**Explicación:** A faster heart rate shortens the diastolic filling period, so the transmitral flow rate and the mean gradient increase. The mean gradient should therefore be reported together with the heart rate.

### 3. Which finding does the ACC/AHA guideline list as a consequence of severe mitral stenosis?

1. PA systolic pressure above 50 mmHg
2. LV dilation
3. A dilated aortic root

**Respuesta del original:** PA systolic pressure above 50 mmHg

**Explicación:** The ACC/AHA guideline lists severe LA enlargement and a PA systolic pressure above 50 mmHg as consequences of severe mitral stenosis. The LV is usually normal in size.
