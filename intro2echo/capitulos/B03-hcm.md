## Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

Septal hypertrophy, SAM and outflow obstruction

In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), the LV wall is thick without another cause, most often at the basal anterior septum. In most patients, systolic anterior motion of the mitral valve obstructs the LV outflow tract, at rest or only with provocation.

**Obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.** The anterior septum measures 20 mm at end-diastole and the posterior wall 9 mm, a ratio of 2.22, above the 1.3 of asymmetric septal hypertrophy in a normotensive patient[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). The peak LVOT velocity is 2.8 m/s at rest, a gradient of 31 mmHg, and 3.3 m/s with Valsalva (44 mmHg): obstruction at rest (30 mmHg or more)[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). At rest, the anterior mitral leaflet meets the septum for 19% of systole (moderate SAM)[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). The leaflets part, with a posteriorly directed MR jet; its vena contracta is 0.38 cm (moderate)[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29). The MR jet reaches 5.7 m/s, faster than the LVOT jet[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). The LVEF is 70%, the LV end-diastolic diameter 39 mm and the LA volume index 34 mL/m² (normal)[14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14). The longer the contact, the higher the gradient[144](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-144).

### Definition and measurement

In an adult, HCM is defined by a maximal end-diastolic wall thickness of 15 mm or more anywhere in the LV, without another cause of hypertrophy[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). A wall of 13–14 mm can be diagnostic in a family member of a patient with HCM or with a positive genetic test[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). The normal septum and posterior wall measure 6–9 mm in women and 6–10 mm in men[14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14).

The most common pattern is focal asymmetric hypertrophy of the basal anterior septum, defined as a septal to posterior wall thickness ratio above 1.3 in a normotensive patient. The variants are a sigmoid septum, reversed septal curvature, and concentric, mid-wall and apical hypertrophy[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). RV trabeculations, the moderator band and the crista supraventricularis are excluded from the septum. A long-axis view can cut the wall tangentially and overestimate it, so the short- and long-axis views are compared[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).

### SAM and dynamic obstruction

Obstruction of the LV outflow tract (LVOT) is caused mainly by systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the mitral valve[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). Drag forces of the flow push the anterior leaflet toward the septum, and SAM can begin at a normal LVOT velocity[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). Most patients with obstruction have abnormal mitral leaflets or papillary muscles; elongated leaflets predispose to SAM[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). SAM is not specific to HCM: a low afterload, a low preload or a high inotropic state can also cause it[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).

On M-mode through the mitral valve, SAM is mild when the leaflet stays more than 10 mm from the septum. It is moderate at 10 mm or less, or with contact for less than 30% of systole, and severe with contact for 30% of systole or more[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). With simultaneous catheterization, SAM without septal contact went with no gradient or a gradient below 10 mmHg, and contact always went with a gradient above 10 mmHg. The earlier the contact began, the longer it lasted and the higher the gradient was: 25 × (the duration of contact ÷ the time from the onset of SAM to contact) + 25 mmHg[144](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-144).

### LVOT gradient and provocation

- The peak LVOT velocity is recorded with CW Doppler, and the gradient is 4 × velocity²[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). Obstruction is present at a peak gradient of 30 mmHg or more[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142), [145](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-145).
- The obstruction is dynamic: it varies with preload, afterload and contractility[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). When the resting peak gradient is below 50 mmHg, the echocardiogram is repeated with provocation: standing, the strain phase of the Valsalva maneuver or exercise[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). The Valsalva effort varies, and its gradient is generally lower than an exercise gradient[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).
- About 30–35% of symptomatic patients have obstruction at rest, and another 30–35% have latent obstruction, present only with provocation[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).
- Exercise echocardiography ( [bonus chapter B1](https://intro2echo.com/stress)) is recommended for symptomatic patients without a resting or provoked gradient of 50 mmHg or more[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142), [145](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-145). A resting or provoked gradient of 50 mmHg or more is the threshold for advanced drug or invasive therapy when symptoms persist despite standard therapy[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142).

### The LVOT jet and the MR jet

- The LVOT velocity rises slowly in early systole, then rises abruptly and peaks in mid-to-late systole, a dagger-shaped envelope[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). In aortic stenosis or a discrete subaortic membrane, the envelope peaks earlier, and the absence of SAM suggests a fixed obstruction[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143) ( [chapter 12](https://intro2echo.com/as)).
- MR usually accompanies SAM, and its jet lies close to the LVOT. The CW beam is swept from the LVOT into the MR jet, and each signal is recorded and labeled[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). Compared with the LVOT jet, the MR jet usually peaks earlier and can last longer, its envelope is rounder, and its velocity is always higher. A velocity above 5.5 m/s is taken as LVOT flow only with caution[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).
- The MR jet is eccentric and directed posteriorly and laterally[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143), and it is typically mid-to-late systolic[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). A central or anterior jet prompts a search for an intrinsic abnormality of the mitral valve, although SAM alone can also produce such a jet[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). The MR varies with the obstruction, so both are assessed at rest and with provocation[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142). The MR is graded as in [chapter 15](https://intro2echo.com/mr): a vena contracta below 0.3 cm is mild and one of 0.7 cm or more is severe[29](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-29).

### LV function, the LA and risk

- The LVEF is normal to hyperdynamic in most patients. An LVEF below 50% defines LV dysfunction[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).
- Four measurements estimate the LA pressure in HCM: an average E/e′ above 14, an LA volume index above 34 mL/m², an Ar−A duration of 30 ms or more and a peak TR velocity above 2.8 m/s[17](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-17) ( [chapter 09](https://intro2echo.com/fill)).
- Three of the major risk factors for sudden death are echocardiographic: a maximal wall thickness of 30 mm or more in any segment, an LV apical aneurysm and an LVEF below 50%[142](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-142).

### Differential diagnosis

Asymmetric hypertrophy and dynamic obstruction are not pathognomonic of HCM[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).

- **Athlete’s heart.** The wall is typically thinner than 15 mm, the four chambers dilate in balance, and the diastolic function is normal or supranormal[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143). A dilated LV with uniform thickening, a normal mitral valve and no obstruction at rest or with exercise favor physiologic hypertrophy[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).
- **Hypertension.** Hypertensive heart disease most commonly gives concentric hypertrophy or remodeling, and the ratio of 1.3 applies to a normotensive patient[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143).
- **Cardiac amyloidosis.** The LV wall thickens concentrically, sometimes more at the septum. Global longitudinal strain falls with relative apical sparing, and the QRS voltage is low for the wall thickness[143](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-143) ( [bonus chapter B4](https://intro2echo.com/amyloid)).

### Key points

1. HCM is a maximal end-diastolic LV wall thickness of 15 mm or more without another cause; 13–14 mm can be diagnostic in a family member or with a positive genetic test.
2. SAM causes dynamic LVOT obstruction; contact with the septum for 30% of systole or more is severe SAM.
3. Obstruction is a peak LVOT gradient of 30 mmHg or more, at rest or with provocation. A resting gradient below 50 mmHg calls for provocation.
4. On CW Doppler, the LVOT jet is dagger-shaped and peaks late. The MR jet peaks earlier, is rounder and is always faster.
