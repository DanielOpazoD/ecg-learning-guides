## Cardiac amyloidosis

Echocardiographic signs

In cardiac amyloidosis, amyloid fibrils accumulate between the myocardial cells. The LV walls thicken around a small cavity, LV filling becomes restrictive, and the base loses longitudinal shortening before the apex. These echocardiographic findings raise the suspicion of cardiac amyloidosis.

The criteria raise the suspicion of cardiac amyloidosis. In the ESC diagnostic pathways, they are used together with other tests[135](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-135).

### Structural findings

- **LV wall thickness.** The LV wall thickness is 12 mm or more in the absence of another cause, such as aortic valve disease or hypertension[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137). Used alone, this cut-off has high sensitivity and low specificity[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137).
- **LV cavity.** The LV cavity is small and the relative wall thickness is high. In a study of 1,187 patients, concentric remodeling was among the best single diagnostic signs[136](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-136).
- **Atria and interatrial septum.** Both atria enlarge; an LA volume index of more than 34 mL/m² is abnormal[14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14). An interatrial septum thicker than 5 mm is characteristic, and the valve leaflets thicken[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137).
- **Right ventricle.** The RV free wall is measured at end-diastole in the parasternal long-axis or subcostal view[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137). A free wall thickness of more than 5 mm indicates RV hypertrophy[33](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-33). RV hypertrophy together with a thick LV wall suggests an infiltrative disease[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137).
- **Pericardium.** A pericardial effusion is present in more than half of patients but is not specific[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137).
- **Myocardial texture.** A granular, “sparkling” or “speckled” myocardium was long described as a sign of amyloidosis. The BSE advises against the term, because other diseases produce the same appearance and harmonic imaging ( [chapter 02](https://intro2echo.com/image)) confounds it[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137).

### Doppler findings

- Diastolic dysfunction progresses from slowed relaxation with a normal filling pressure (grade 1) to restrictive filling with an E/A ratio of 2 or more (grade 3)[17](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-17) ( [chapter 09](https://intro2echo.com/fill)).
- A clear restrictive filling pattern seldom appears before the late stages of the disease[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137). In these stages, the deceleration time falls below 150 ms and the septal and lateral e′ velocities fall to 3–4 cm/s[17](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-17).
- The “5-5-5” sign, in which the septal s′, e′ and a′ velocities are all below 5 cm/s, is one of the three findings of the ESC echocardiographic criteria[135](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-135).
- A small A wave suggests reduced atrial function and a higher risk of atrial thrombus[137](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-137).

### Longitudinal strain

- Longitudinal strain is reduced most at the base. On the bull’s-eye plot ( [chapter 10](https://intro2echo.com/segments)), the apical segments remain red while the basal segments become pale, a pattern termed relative apical sparing[138](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-138).
- Relative apical LS is a ratio of regional strain values: the mean apical strain divided by the sum of the mean basal and mean mid-ventricular strain. In the original study, a value of 1.0 or more distinguished amyloidosis from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and aortic stenosis with a sensitivity of 93% and a specificity of 82%[138](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-138).
- A multicenter study found apical sparing in 32% of patients in whom amyloidosis was suspected and then excluded, and in 6% of healthy individuals[139](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-139).
- The base-to-apex strain gradient corresponds to the total amyloid mass in each region, which is largest at the base[140](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-140).
- An EF ÷ \|GLS\| ratio of more than 4.1 distinguished amyloidosis from other causes of increased LV wall thickness with a sensitivity of 89.7% and a specificity of 91.7%[141](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-141). A normal EF can mask impaired long-axis function.
- GLS is normal when more negative than −18% and abnormal when less negative than −16%[39](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-39) ( [chapter 08](https://intro2echo.com/eject)). The ESC criteria use a stricter cut-off: an absolute GLS below 15%[135](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-135).

### ESC 2021 echocardiographic criteria

The criteria require an unexplained LV wall thickness of 12 mm or more together with either two of the three findings or a score of 8 or more[135](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-135); the diagram above applies them to the values on screen. The score assigns points for a thick wall around a small cavity, a high E/e′, a low TAPSE ( [chapter 08](https://intro2echo.com/eject)), a low GLS and relative sparing of the apical septum[136](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-136). The criteria raise the suspicion of cardiac amyloidosis. In the ESC diagnostic pathways, they are used together with other tests[135](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-135).

### Measurement

- Wall thickness is measured at end-diastole, perpendicular to the LV long axis, at the level of the mitral leaflet tips[14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14).
- In the score, relative wall thickness is calculated as (IVS + PW) ÷ LVIDd[135](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-135); the ASE guideline defines it as 2 × PW ÷ LVIDd, with a cut-off of 0.42[14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14).

### Key points

1. An LV wall thickness of 12 mm or more without another cause, a small LV cavity and enlargement of both atria suggest cardiac amyloidosis.
2. Restrictive filling, septal s′, e′ and a′ velocities below 5 cm/s and relative apical sparing of longitudinal strain support the diagnosis.
3. The echocardiographic criteria raise the suspicion of cardiac amyloidosis but do not establish the diagnosis alone.
