## Transcatheter aortic valve replacement

TAVR, step by step: what TEE shows at each step

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) places a valve on a metal frame inside the stenotic aortic valve, brought on a catheter from the femoral artery. Fluoroscopy guides the procedure, TEE helps at each step, and TTE shows the result.

### The device

A bioprosthesis on a metal frame is crimped onto a catheter and expanded inside the stenotic native valve. A balloon-expandable valve, the SAPIEN 3 (a cobalt-chromium frame with bovine pericardial leaflets), opens with one balloon inflation under rapid ventricular pacing[77](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-77). A self-expanding valve, the Evolut (a nitinol frame with porcine pericardial leaflets[78](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-78) that sit above the annulus[79](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-79)), is released slowly from its capsule without rapid pacing[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80), and can be recaptured before its release[78](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-78). From 2019 to 2022, 96.2% of TAVRs in the US registry used transfemoral access[81](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-81). The figure shows a 26-mm SAPIEN 3.

### Who is treated

TAVR lowered the 1-year mortality of patients who could not have surgery from 50.7% with standard therapy to 30.7%[82](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-82), and the 1-year death, stroke or rehospitalization of patients at low surgical risk from 15.1% after surgery to 8.5%[83](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-83). The 2025 ESC/EACTS guideline recommends TAVR from 70 years of age with a tricuspid valve and suitable anatomy, and surgery below 70 at low surgical risk[84](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-84).

### The anatomy for TAVR

The annulus that sizes the valve is the diameter at the hinge points of the cusps, measured in systole[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85): from the right cusp's hinge anteriorly to the base of the interleaflet trigone posteriorly, inner edge to inner edge, on a zoomed long axis[86](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-86). A TEE annulus of 21–25 mm takes a 26-mm SAPIEN 3[77](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-77), and an annulus of 23–26 mm a 29-mm Evolut R[78](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-78). CT is the reference for sizing; 3D TEE measures 3.5–7.4% smaller, usually without a change of valve size[87](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-87). A left main height below 10–11 mm warns of coronary obstruction[86](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-86).

### The steps on TEE

Fluoroscopy guides most of the procedure; TEE or TTE helps to place the pacing wire, the guidewire and the valve, and checks the result at once[88](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-88). TEE gives continuous images, but needs sedation or anesthesia, and its probe can obstruct fluoroscopy[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80). Conscious sedation for transfemoral TAVR rose from 33.4% to 64.1% of US cases between 2016 and 2019[89](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-89). The EACVI prefers this minimalist approach with TTE, converting to TEE for complications; TEE suits new programs and patients at higher risk[87](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-87). The views below follow EACVI 2023[87](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-87) ( [chapter 17](https://intro2echo.com/tee)).

1. **Measure.** The midesophageal long axis of the aortic valve, at 120–140°, and its short axis, at 30–60°, are often acquired as a biplane image[87](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-87). The long axis gives the annulus, the short axis the cusps and their calcium, and a 3D zoom of the root the annular area, its perimeter and the left main height[86](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-86). The deep transgastric view aligns the Doppler beam with the valve for the baseline gradient[90](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-90).
2. **Cross.** TEE confirms the pacing wire in the RV[87](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-87) and, on the mitral biplane views, watches the stiff wire in the LV for entanglement in the mitral apparatus, its commonest complication: a new change in the mitral leaflets or in MR[91](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-91). Echo places the crimped valve by the hinge points of the cusps, which can lie slightly aortic to the fluoroscopic landmark[91](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-91).
3. **Deploy.** A SAPIEN 3 is placed for a final depth of 80/20 to 90/10 of its frame height, aortic to ventricular[92](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-92). Its frame shortens as it expands, mostly at its ventricular end: from 27 to 20 mm for the 26-mm valve[93](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-93). With the cusp-overlap technique, an Evolut targets 3 mm below the noncoronary cusp, and is recaptured at less than 1 mm or more than 5 mm[94](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-94).
4. **Check.** Right after the implant, TEE confirms that all the prosthetic cusps move, that the frame is circular and that there is no significant valvular or paravalvular regurgitation; central regurgitation is common while the wire crosses the valve and for minutes after, as the crimped leaflets recover. Transgastric views with color, PW and CW Doppler complete the check before the probe is removed[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85).

The whole valve is scanned from its aortic to its ventricular end. A paravalvular jet arises at the frame's ventricular edge and counts only when it enters the LV; color in the sinuses above the skirt is not a leak[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80). Its circumferential extent is the sum of the jets' vena contracta arcs divided by the circumference of the valve's outer edge: less than 10% is mild, 10–29% moderate and 30% or more severe, best read with the vena contracta[71](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-71). The frame shadows posterior jets on TTE and anterior jets on TEE; the deep transgastric view finds anterior jets that the esophageal views miss. The regurgitant volume and fraction cannot be measured during the procedure[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80). With the SAPIEN 3, at least moderate paravalvular regurgitation at 30 days carried a hazard ratio of 2.40 for death at 1 year, and mild regurgitation did not raise mortality; at least moderate regurgitation is corrected at the procedure, by balloon post-dilatation for example[95](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-95).

### After the procedure

A TTE soon after the implant sets the baseline[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80). VARC-3 defines the intended performance of the valve as a mean gradient below 20 mmHg, a peak velocity below 3 m/s, a DVI of 0.25 or more and less than moderate regurgitation[96](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-96). For the EOA, the LVOT diameter is measured outer edge to outer edge at the ventricular end of the frame, with the PW sample just proximal to the flow acceleration at the frame's inlet; a sample inside the frame overestimates the EOA[71](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-71). The normal values depend on the valve and its size: a mean gradient of 10.6 mmHg and an EOA of 1.74 cm² for a 26-mm SAPIEN 3, and 7.9 mmHg and 1.97 cm² for a 29-mm Evolut R[71](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-71); mismatch is graded from the indexed EOA, as after surgery ( [bonus chapter B6](https://intro2echo.com/prosthetic)). Later, a mean gradient that rises by 10 mmHg or more from the study at 1–3 months, to 20 mmHg or more, with a fall of the EOA or the DVI, is moderate hemodynamic valve deterioration[96](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-96).

A periaortic hematoma, a fast-growing pericardial effusion and atypical flow from the annulus indicate annular rupture[91](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-91). A new regional wall-motion abnormality suggests coronary obstruction[87](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-87). New MR can come from RV pacing, from a low valve that presses on the anterior mitral leaflet, or from damage to the subvalvular apparatus[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85). A perforation of the RV by the pacing wire can appear only after the wire's removal[91](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-91).

### Key points

1. The annulus is measured in systole at the cusps' hinge points, inner edge to inner edge[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85), [86](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-86).
2. A SAPIEN 3 is placed for a final depth of 80/20 to 90/10; its frame shortens mostly at its ventricular end[92](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-92), [93](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-93).
3. A paravalvular jet counts where it enters the LVOT; an extent below 10% of the circumference is mild, 10–29% moderate and 30% or more severe[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80), [71](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-71).
4. Intended performance: a mean gradient below 20 mmHg, a peak velocity below 3 m/s, a DVI of 0.25 or more and less than moderate regurgitation[96](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-96).

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. Where is the aortic annulus measured on TEE to size a transcatheter valve?

1. At the sinotubular junction, in diastole
2. At the hinge points of the cusps, in systole, inner edge to inner edge
3. At the tips of the open cusps, in systole

**Respuesta del original:** At the hinge points of the cusps, in systole, inner edge to inner edge

**Explicación:** The annulus that sizes the valve is the diameter at the hinge points of the cusps, measured in systole on a zoomed long axis, inner edge to inner edge (ASE 2022). CT is the reference for sizing; 3D TEE measures 3.5–7.4% smaller, usually without a change of valve size (EACVI 2023).

### 2. A 26-mm SAPIEN 3 sits across the annulus before its balloon is inflated. What final depth is the target?

1. 80/20 to 90/10 of the frame's height, aortic to ventricular
2. 50/50: the middle of the frame at the annulus
3. The whole frame below the annulus, in the LVOT

**Respuesta del original:** 80/20 to 90/10 of the frame's height, aortic to ventricular

**Explicación:** A SAPIEN 3 is placed for a final depth of 80/20 to 90/10 of its frame's height, aortic to ventricular (Blackman 2021). The frame shortens as it expands, mostly at its ventricular end, from 27 to 20 mm for the 26-mm valve (Schymik 2015), so that end rises toward the annulus as the balloon fills.

### 3. At the check, two paravalvular jets take 20% of the frame's circumference. What is the grade?

1. Mild
2. Moderate
3. Severe

**Respuesta del original:** Moderate

**Explicación:** The circumferential extent is the sum of the jets' vena contracta arcs divided by the circumference of the valve's outer edge: less than 10% is mild, 10–29% moderate and 30% or more severe, best read with the vena contracta (ASE 2024). A jet counts only where it enters the LV at the frame's ventricular edge; color in the sinuses above the skirt is not a leak (ASE 2019).

### 4. Which result meets the valve's intended performance in VARC-3?

1. A mean gradient of 25 mmHg and a DVI of 0.40
2. A mean gradient of 10 mmHg, a peak velocity of 2.2 m/s, a DVI of 0.46 and mild paravalvular regurgitation
3. A peak velocity of 3.4 m/s with no regurgitation

**Respuesta del original:** A mean gradient of 10 mmHg, a peak velocity of 2.2 m/s, a DVI of 0.46 and mild paravalvular regurgitation

**Explicación:** VARC-3 asks for a mean gradient below 20 mmHg, a peak velocity below 3 m/s, a DVI of 0.25 or more and less than moderate regurgitation (Généreux 2021). Mild paravalvular regurgitation meets it; with the SAPIEN 3, at least moderate regurgitation at 30 days carried a hazard ratio of 2.40 for death at 1 year (Pibarot 2017).
