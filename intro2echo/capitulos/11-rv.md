## Right ventricle

RV size and function, pressure and volume overload, acute pulmonary embolism

The RV is assessed by its size, its systolic function and the pressure that it generates. The septum flattens at end-systole in pressure overload and at end-diastole only in volume overload; in acute pulmonary embolism the RV dilates while its wall stays thin.

### RV size

The RV is measured at end-diastole in the RV-focused apical four-chamber view; at least one measure of RV size, typically the basal diameter just beneath the tricuspid annulus, should be reported[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32). In the four-chamber view, the RV is normally no more than two-thirds the size of the LV, and the LV forms the apex[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

- The RV basal diameter is normal below 4.1 cm, mildly dilated at 4.1–4.4 cm, moderately at 4.5–4.9 cm and severely above 4.9 cm[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).
- The RV free wall is normally thinner than 5 mm at end-diastole; a thick wall is not specific, as it occurs in pulmonary hypertension and in infiltrative and inherited cardiomyopathies[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).
- The RV area is normal below 25 cm² at end-diastole and below 14 cm² at end-systole[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).
- The RA area at end-systole is normal below 19 cm²[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

### RV systolic function

The ASE guideline grades these measures of RV systolic function[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32):

- **TAPSE.** TAPSE is the M-mode excursion of the lateral tricuspid annulus toward the apex[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4). It is normal above 17 mm.
- **S′.** S′ is the systolic tissue Doppler velocity of the same point. It is normal above 9.5 cm/s.
- **Fractional area change.** The fractional area change (FAC) is (end-diastolic area − end-systolic area) ÷ end-diastolic area. It is normal above 35%[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).
- **Free-wall strain.** The longitudinal strain of the RV free wall is normal when it is more negative than −20%[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

TAPSE and S′ measure the motion of one point of the annulus; FAC comes from the whole RV area in one plane, and so includes the radial motion of the free wall and the septum[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32). In a pressure-overloaded RV that has begun to fail, the base often keeps its motion, so TAPSE and S′ can overestimate RV function[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

### Pressure overload

The RV systolic pressure is 4 × the peak TR velocity² + the RA pressure ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)), which comes from the IVC ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)); it equals the PA systolic pressure when there is no RVOT obstruction or pulmonic stenosis[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32). The ASE guideline grades it as normal up to 34 mmHg, mildly raised at 35–49 mmHg, moderately at 50–69 mmHg and severely at 70 mmHg or more[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

As the resistance of the pulmonary vessels rises, the RVOT acceleration time, normally above 105 ms, shortens, and the RVOT flow can notch in mid-systole, the W sign[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

The RV first adapts with concentric hypertrophy and a preserved ejection fraction. When it maladapts, it dilates, its function falls and its shape changes from a crescent to a sphere; the tricuspid annulus can then dilate, with functional TR[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

### Volume overload

Significant TR or PR, an atrial septal defect and anomalous pulmonary venous drainage put a volume load on the RV, and the right heart dilates[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32). In severe TR, the TR velocity can underestimate the RV–RA gradient[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32) ( [chapter 16](https://intro2echo.com/tr)).

### The septum

The LV eccentricity index (LVEI) is the ratio of two diameters of the LV in the short axis at the papillary muscles: D2, parallel to the septum, over D1, from the septum across the LV[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32). In a normal heart the LV is round, and the index is about 1 at end-diastole and at end-systole[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32), [181](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-181).

- An index above 1 at end-diastole only suggests RV volume overload.
- An index above 1 at end-systole suggests RV pressure overload.
- An index above 1 at both times suggests RV pressure overload, with or without volume overload[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

Conduction abnormalities, pacing, cardiac surgery and constriction can change the motion of the septum[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32).

### Acute pulmonary embolism

Acute pulmonary embolism puts a sudden pressure load on an RV that has not hypertrophied: it enlarges before the pulmonary pressures rise, and its wall thickens only later[33](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-33). The ESC guideline shows the signs of RV pressure overload: a basal RV/LV diameter ratio above 1.0, a flattened septum, a distended IVC with less inspiratory collapse, a TAPSE below 16 mm, an S′ below 9.5 cm/s, the 60/60 sign, McConnell's sign and a mobile thrombus in the right heart[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182).

McConnell's sign is akinesia of the mid RV free wall with normal motion at the apex; in the first series, the mid free wall moved −0.2 ± 0.8 mm[183](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-183). The 60/60 sign is a pulmonary ejection acceleration time, measured in the RVOT, below 60 ms with a peak systolic TR gradient below 60 mmHg[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182). Both signs are specific but insensitive[184](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-184); pooled over 11 studies, McConnell's sign had a sensitivity of 29.1% and a specificity of 98.6%[185](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-185). McConnell's sign also occurs in RV infarction[172](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-172) ( [bonus chapter B10](https://intro2echo.com/mi)); signs of RV pressure overload help to distinguish acute PE from RV infarction[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182).

A peak TR velocity above 3.8 m/s, a TR gradient above 60 mmHg or a thick RV wall goes beyond what an acute pressure load produces, and chronic thromboembolic or other pulmonary hypertension should then be considered[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182). The figure's pressure load is that chronic picture: its RV wall thickens to 9.5 mm and its TR velocity reaches 4.4 m/s on the normal heart, against 4.0 mm and 3.3 m/s or less under acute PE.

Echocardiography is not mandatory in a hemodynamically stable patient with suspected PE, and a normal study does not exclude PE: its negative predictive value is 40–50%[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182). In suspected high-risk PE with hemodynamic instability, the absence of RV overload or dysfunction practically excludes PE as the cause. Unequivocal RV pressure overload with no other obvious cause justifies emergency reperfusion when CT angiography is not feasible, in a patient with a high clinical probability[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182). An RV/LV ratio of 1.0 or more and a TAPSE below 16 mm are the findings most often linked to an unfavorable prognosis[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182); the grades of the figure are echocardiographic findings, not risk classes.

### The probability of pulmonary hypertension

Pulmonary hypertension is defined by a mean PA pressure above 20 mmHg at rest. Echocardiography gives a probability of PH. The ESC/ERS guideline uses the peak TR velocity, not the estimated PA systolic pressure, because the estimate of the RA pressure adds error:

- A peak TR velocity of 2.8 m/s or less, or no measurable velocity, gives a low probability, or an intermediate one with other signs.
- A velocity of 2.9–3.4 m/s gives an intermediate probability, or a high one with other signs.
- A velocity above 3.4 m/s gives a high probability.

The other signs are in three categories. The ventricles: an RV/LV basal ratio above 1.0, a flat septum with an LVEI above 1.1, and a TAPSE/sPAP ratio below 0.55 mm/mmHg. The pulmonary artery: an RVOT acceleration time below 105 ms or mid-systolic notching, an early-diastolic PR velocity above 2.2 m/s, and a PA wider than the aortic root or wider than 25 mm. The IVC and the RA: an IVC wider than 21 mm with a reduced collapse, and an RA area above 18 cm². Signs change the probability only when they come from at least two categories. The ASE guideline calls a TR velocity of 2.9 m/s or more, or 2.8 m/s with at least two other signs, suggestive of PH.

The signs are not specific to a pressure load. In the figure, a volume load reaches an intermediate probability with a normal TR velocity, through the RV/LV ratio, the septum at end-diastole, the PA diameter and the RA area.

### Key points

1. At least one measure each of RV size, typically the basal diameter[32](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-32), and of RV function should be reported[36](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-36).
2. The septum flattens at end-systole in pressure overload and at end-diastole only in volume overload.
3. The probability of PH comes from the peak TR velocity and from signs in at least two of three categories: the ventricles, the pulmonary artery, and the IVC and the RA.
4. In a failing, pressure-overloaded RV, TAPSE and S′ can overestimate RV function; FAC also shows the radial motion of the free wall and the septum.
5. In acute pulmonary embolism the RV dilates while its wall stays thin and the TR gradient stays at 60 mmHg or less. McConnell's sign and the 60/60 sign are specific but insensitive, and a normal echocardiogram does not exclude PE[182](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-182), [184](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-184).

### 1. Which measure of RV systolic function is derived from the whole RV area in one plane?

1. TAPSE
2. S′
3. Fractional area change

**Respuesta del original:** Fractional area change

**Explicación:** TAPSE and S′ measure the motion of a single point of the tricuspid annulus; FAC compares the RV areas at end-diastole and end-systole.

### 2. In the short axis at the papillary muscles, the LV eccentricity index is 1.3 at end-diastole and 1.0 at end-systole. What does this pattern suggest?

1. RV volume overload
2. RV pressure overload
3. A normal septum

**Respuesta del original:** RV volume overload

**Explicación:** A septum flat at end-diastole only suggests RV volume overload. A septum flat at end-systole suggests RV pressure overload.
