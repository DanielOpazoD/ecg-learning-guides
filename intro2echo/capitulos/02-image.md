## Image

Image formation and artifacts

A two-dimensional echocardiographic sector image is formed from many scan lines acquired in sequence. Imaging depth, sector width and line density determine the number of frames that the ultrasound system can acquire each second.

At an imaging depth of 16 cm, the echo from the deepest point of each scan line returns after 208 µs. A frame of 96 scan lines takes 19.9 ms, which corresponds to a frame rate of **50 frames per second**.

### Frame rate

time per line ≈ 2 × depth ÷ c  ·  time per frame = lines × time per line

The next pulse is transmitted only after the echoes from the deepest point of the current scan line have returned[1](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-1). A greater number of scan lines, a greater depth or more focal zones increase the time required to acquire each frame. In an example in the ASE guideline, a depth of 17 cm with a narrow sector gave 84 frames per second, and a depth of 24 cm with a wide sector gave 43[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4).

The frame rate increases when the depth is reduced, the sector is narrowed, fewer focal zones are used or write zoom is applied[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4). Because the heart moves rapidly, temporal resolution is important in echocardiography[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4).

M-mode repeats a single scan line and displays its echoes against time. Its temporal resolution is far higher than that of a 2D image, so it shows rapidly moving structures best[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4). M-mode measures the IVC ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)) and TAPSE ( [chapter 08](https://intro2echo.com/eject)), and it times the RA wall inversion in tamponade ( [bonus chapter B5](https://intro2echo.com/peri)).

### Lateral resolution

Lateral resolution, the resolution perpendicular to the beam, depends on the beam width. The beam is narrowest at the focus, and lateral resolution is typically 1 to 2 mm[1](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-1). The focus should be placed at the depth of the structure of interest[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4). More scan lines, spaced more closely, improve lateral resolution but lower the frame rate[5](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-5).

### Artifacts

The ultrasound system assumes that the pulse travels in a straight line and is reflected only once, that every echo originates on the beam axis, and that each echo returns after a time proportional to the depth of its reflector[6](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-6). Each artifact arises when one of these assumptions is violated.

- **Reverberation.** Ultrasound is reflected back and forth between two strong reflectors. Copies of the deeper reflector appear at 2 and 3 times the distance between the reflectors, measured from the shallower reflector[6](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-6).
- **Shadowing.** A strong reflector or attenuator, such as a prosthetic ring or calcium, eliminates the echoes from the region behind it[6](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-6).
- **Mirror image.** A strong reflector, often the pleura, produces a copy of the structures in front of it at a position behind it[6](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-6).
- **Side lobe.** Echoes of off-axis energy from a strong reflector are displayed as if they originated in the main beam, often as an arc[6](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-6).

Harmonic imaging forms the image from echoes at twice the transmit frequency and is recommended by the ASE[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4). It reduces side-lobe artifacts[7](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-7), but it can make valve leaflets appear thicker[8](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-8).

### Key points

1. The frame rate equals 1 ÷ (number of scan lines × time per line).
2. A reduction in imaging depth and sector width increases the frame rate.
3. Each artifact can be identified from the assumption of image formation that it violates.

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. An image is acquired at a depth of 16 cm with 100 scan lines and one focal zone. What is the approximate frame rate?

1. Approximately 12 frames per second
2. Approximately 48 frames per second
3. Approximately 200 frames per second

**Respuesta del original:** Approximately 48 frames per second

**Explicación:** Each scan line takes 2 × 0.16 m ÷ 1540 m/s ≈ 208 µs. A frame of 100 lines therefore takes approximately 21 ms, which corresponds to approximately 48 frames per second.

### 2. Which change in the imaging settings increases the frame rate?

1. A greater imaging depth
2. A narrower sector
3. More focal zones

**Respuesta del original:** A narrower sector

**Explicación:** A narrower sector requires fewer scan lines for each frame, so each frame takes less time to acquire.

### 3. A second image of the heart appears behind the bright pleural line. Which artifact is this?

1. Reverberation
2. Mirror image
3. Shadowing

**Respuesta del original:** Mirror image

**Explicación:** The pleura acts as an acoustic mirror and produces a copy of the structures in front of it at a position behind it.
