## Shunts: ASD, VSD and PFO

Septal defects, Qp/Qs and the saline study

A septal defect or a patent foramen ovale lets blood pass between the left and the right heart. Echocardiography shows the defect and its flow, measures Qp/Qs, and grades its effect on the chambers and the pulmonary pressure. Across a restrictive atrial communication, the shunt velocity gives the LA pressure.

### Atrial septal defect

Secundum defects, at the fossa ovalis, make up 80% of ASDs; sinus venosus defects in general require TEE[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126). The shunt is in general relevant from a defect size of 10 mm[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126). RV volume overload is the key finding, and it characterizes the hemodynamic relevance of the defect better than the shunt ratio[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126). The RA and the RV dilate, and the ventricular septum flattens in diastole[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173) ( [chapter 11](https://intro2echo.com/rv)).

The subcostal four-chamber view is preferred: the septum lies nearly perpendicular to the beam, so a true defect is told from dropout and measured[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173). In the apical four-chamber view the septum lies parallel to the beam, so an ASD should not be diagnosed or measured there; that view shows the RA and RV dilation and gives the RV pressure from the TR jet[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173). The diameter is measured without color, which can overestimate the defect, and the color scale is lowered to 25–40 cm/s for the low-velocity shunt flow[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173).

### Ventricular septal defect

Perimembranous defects, next to the tricuspid and aortic valves, make up 80% of VSDs[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126). CW Doppler through the defect gives the LV–RV gradient as 4V², and the RV systolic pressure is the LV or aortic systolic pressure minus that gradient[174](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-174). The figure takes a systolic blood pressure of 125 mmHg for the aortic systolic pressure.

A small VSD with an insignificant left-to-right shunt, no LV volume overload and no pulmonary hypertension is restrictive[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126), and its jet is fast[175](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-175). A large shunt loads the LA and the LV with volume[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126). The figure calls the LV enlarged above an LVEDD of 58 mm and the LA above a volume index of 34 mL/m², the upper limits of normal for men[14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14). Aortic regurgitation from cusp prolapse should be sought, especially with outlet and high perimembranous defects[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126).

### Qp/Qs and closure

Qp/Qs is the RVOT stroke volume ÷ the LVOT stroke volume[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173) ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)), and the calculation can have an error of up to 20%[9](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-9). A shunt is hemodynamically significant when the chambers distal to it are enlarged and/or Qp:Qs is 1.5:1 or more; otherwise it is small or trivial[162](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-162). The figure takes the RV basal diameter as the chamber for an ASD, and the LVEDD for a VSD.

The AHA/ACC guideline recommends closure of an isolated secundum ASD that impairs functional capacity, with RA and/or RV enlargement and a net left-to-right shunt with physiological sequelae (for example Qp:Qs of 1.5:1 or more) without cyanosis; in an adult without symptoms but with these findings, closure is reasonable[162](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-162). It recommends closure of a VSD with LV volume overload and a Qp:Qs of 1.5:1 or more. For both, the condition is a systolic PA pressure below 50% of the systemic pressure and a pulmonary vascular resistance below one third of the systemic resistance; with a VSD and a systolic PA pressure of 50% or more, closure may be considered[162](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-162). An ASD should not be closed with a systolic PA pressure or a pulmonary vascular resistance above two thirds of the systemic value, and/or a net right-to-left shunt[162](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-162). In an ASD, a calculated systolic PA pressure above 40 mmHg requires catheterization to measure the pulmonary vascular resistance[126](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-126).

### Patent foramen ovale and the saline study

Agitated saline forms microbubbles too large to pass the normal pulmonary vessels, so bubbles in the left heart have crossed a shunt[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173), [4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4). Bubbles in the LA within 3–6 beats of the first beat with the RA and the RV fully opacified[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4) indicate an intracardiac shunt such as a PFO; later bubbles, from the pulmonary veins, indicate an intrapulmonary shunt such as an arteriovenous malformation[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173).

A Valsalva maneuver or a cough is usually needed to show a PFO that does not shunt at rest[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173). The strain is released when the contrast arrives in the RA[4](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-4); a shift of the atrial septum toward the LA at the release shows that the RA pressure exceeded the LA pressure[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173). An inadequate Valsalva maneuver or RA opacification gives false negatives, and TEE is the reference standard[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173). The number of bubbles in one frame depends on the contrast injected and the maneuver[173](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-173). In patients aged 18 to 60 years with a prior PFO-associated stroke, the SCAI guideline recommends PFO closure rather than antiplatelet therapy alone[176](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-176).

### LA pressure from an atrial shunt

A restrictive atrial communication keeps a pressure difference between the atria. Across it, CW Doppler gives the LA–RA gradient as 4V², so the LA pressure is the RA pressure + 4V²[177](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-177), [178](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-178). After transseptal mitral edge-to-edge repair, the mean gradient + the central venous pressure gave the mean LA pressure[177](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-177), and the peak gradient + the RA pressure gave the peak LA pressure, with limits of agreement of −6.2 to +3.1 mmHg[178](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-178). Both studies used TEE and a catheter RA pressure[177](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-177), [178](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-178). On TTE the RA pressure comes from the IVC ( [chapter 07](https://intro2echo.com/pressure)), and its error adds directly to the LA pressure.

A large ASD lets the atrial pressures equalize: in ASD the mean transatrial septal velocity was 41 ± 11 cm/s[179](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-179), a gradient of about 1 mmHg, so the shunt cannot show a raised LA pressure. A PFO can shunt left to right when a left-sided lesion raises the LA pressure: in one series, every PFO that did had an LA pressure above 13 mmHg and an LA–RA gradient above 10 mmHg[180](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-180).

The figure takes a 5-mm communication and an RA pressure of 3 mmHg, from a normal IVC, and its slider sets the mean LA pressure; the pressure waveforms are teaching values. It calls a mean LA pressure above 15 mmHg raised, the cut-off for an abnormally elevated LV filling pressure in the study that validated the algorithm of [chapter 09](https://intro2echo.com/fill) [43](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-43).

### Key points

1. An ASD is measured in the subcostal four-chamber view. A shunt is hemodynamically significant when it enlarges the chambers distal to it and/or Qp:Qs is 1.5 or more.
2. Across a VSD, the RV systolic pressure is the systemic systolic pressure minus 4V² of the jet.
3. On the saline study, LA bubbles within 3–6 beats of full RA opacification indicate an intracardiac shunt such as a PFO; Valsalva release can show a shunt absent at rest.
4. Across a restrictive atrial communication, the LA pressure is the RA pressure + 4V²; across a large ASD the atrial pressures equalize.

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. Across a restrictive atrial communication, the peak septal velocity is 2.0 m/s, and the RA pressure is 8 mmHg. What is the peak LA pressure?

1. 16 mmHg
2. 24 mmHg
3. 32 mmHg

**Respuesta del original:** 24 mmHg

**Explicación:** The peak LA–RA gradient is 4 × 2.0² = 16 mmHg. The peak LA pressure is the RA pressure + the gradient: 8 + 16 = 24 mmHg.

### 2. Why can the flow across a large secundum ASD not show a raised LA pressure?

1. The flow is right to left
2. The atrial pressures equalize, so the gradient stays at about 1 mmHg
3. CW Doppler cannot record flow across the atrial septum

**Respuesta del original:** The atrial pressures equalize, so the gradient stays at about 1 mmHg

**Explicación:** A large defect lets the LA and RA pressures equalize. In ASD the mean transatrial septal velocity is about 0.4 m/s, a gradient of about 1 mmHg, so the LA pressure is close to the RA pressure. Only a restrictive communication keeps a gradient that gives the LA pressure.
