## Mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair

M-TEER, step by step: what TEE shows at each step

Mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) joins the anterior and posterior mitral leaflets with a clip, brought from the femoral vein across the atrial septum. TEE guides each step of the procedure, and TTE shows the result.

### The device

Two devices are approved, each with its own implantation system[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). A steerable guide catheter enters the femoral vein and runs up the inferior vena cava to the right atrium[98](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-98); over a wire it crosses the septum, and about 2 cm of it lies in the LA[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The delivery system goes through the guide into the LA and carries the implant[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The implant has two arms (paddles in one device), with a gripper (a clasp) over each, and the grippers drop together or one at a time[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). Each device comes in more than one size: a longer one for a larger gap, a shorter and narrower one for a grasp away from the middle of the valve[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).

### Who is treated

EVEREST II randomly assigned 279 patients with grade 3+ or 4+ MR to TEER or to surgery[99](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-99); most had primary MR[98](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-98). At 12 months, freedom from death, from surgery for mitral dysfunction and from grade 3+ or 4+ MR was 55% after TEER and 73% after surgery; major adverse events at 30 days were 15% and 48%[99](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-99). The FDA approved the first mitral TEER device in 2013 for primary MR at prohibitive surgical risk[98](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-98), and for secondary MR in 2019[100](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-100).

In secondary MR, COAPT (614 patients) lowered the rate of heart failure hospitalization within 24 months, 35.8% against 67.9% per patient-year, and death, 29.1% against 46.1%[101](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-101). MITRA-FR (304 patients) found no difference in death or heart failure hospitalization at 12 months[102](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-102). RESHAPE-HF2 (505 patients, moderate to severe secondary MR) lowered the rate of heart failure hospitalization or cardiovascular death, 37.0 against 58.9 events per 100 patient-years[103](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-103).

The 2020 ACC/AHA guideline finds TEER reasonable in severely symptomatic (NYHA III–IV) patients with severe primary MR at high or prohibitive surgical risk, with favorable anatomy and a life expectancy of at least 1 year; and in chronic severe secondary MR with persistent symptoms on optimal therapy, an anatomy suitable on TEE, an LVEF of 20–50%, an LVESD of 70 mm or less and a PA systolic pressure of 70 mmHg or less[38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38). In the 2025 ESC/EACTS guidelines, TEER should be considered in symptomatic severe primary MR with a suitable anatomy at high surgical risk, and it is recommended in severe ventricular secondary MR that meets their clinical and echocardiographic criteria[84](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-84).

### The anatomy for TEER

In EVEREST, the jet came from A2–P2; a valve area under 4.0 cm² excluded the patient; in primary MR the flail gap was under 10 mm and the flail width under 15 mm, and in secondary MR the coaptation length was at least 2 mm and its depth 11 mm or less[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85). A baseline valve area of 3.0 cm² or less with a mean gradient of 4 mmHg or more carried a high risk of a procedure stopped for stenosis[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80).

### The steps on TEE

1. **Transseptal puncture.** The sheath tents the fossa ovalis. The bicaval view places the tent between the venae cavae, the short-axis view at the aortic valve to the front or back, and the four-chamber view gives its height above the point where the clip will grasp: about 4.5–5.0 cm[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). With a flail or prolapse, that point lies at or a little above the annulus[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The needle goes through the posterior-mid fossa, in a posterior and superior direction[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85).
2. **Steering and alignment.** The clip goes down over the jet's origin and stops about 1 cm above the valve[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85), [97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The commissural view (55–75°) shows its medial-lateral position, and the long-axis view (100–160°) its anterior-posterior position[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85). With the arms open, the clip turns until they are square to the line of coaptation[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The arms then lie in the long-axis plane: that view shows both, and the commissural view shows them edge-on, as one line. 3D en face and transgastric views show the turn[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85).
3. **Grasping.** The clip crosses below the leaflet tips with its arms open or closed; there the arms open, and the clip comes back until the leaflets lie on them[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The grippers drop onto the arms, together or one at a time, and the clip closes[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The leaflets drape over the arms, the grasped tissue stops moving, and 3D shows a tissue bridge[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). Leaflet length from the annulus, before and after the grasp, estimates how much leaflet the clip holds[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).
4. **Assessment.** With the settings used before the clip, the commissural view shows the jets on both sides of it[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80). Two or three thin jets are usually no more than mild MR, and PISA is not recommended after TEER[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80), [97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). A mean gradient under 5 mmHg with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more is preferred, reported with the heart rate[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). In 268 patients, most with secondary MR, an invasive gradient above 5 mmHg just after implantation predicted a worse long-term outcome[104](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-104).
5. **Release.** Before release, the MR reduction is weighed against the rise in gradient[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The clip is then detached from the delivery system[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85). After release it often moves 1–2 mm toward the LV, and it can turn a little[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).

The 3D en face view from the LA shows the valve as the surgeon sees it, with the aortic valve at 12 o'clock and the LA appendage on the left[105](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-105).

### After the procedure

After the procedure TTE is the main study[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80); at 6 months it shows the LV's remodeling[85](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-85). A vena contracta of 0.7 cm or more in any jet is specific for severe residual MR; with several jets, 3D TEE adds the jets' vena contracta areas[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80) ( [chapter 15](https://intro2echo.com/mr)). In one series of 66 patients the iatrogenic ASD persisted in 50% at 6 months, and its persistence went with a higher mortality[106](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-106); routine closure is not recommended[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97). The peak velocity across it gives the LA–RA pressure difference, 4v² ( [bonus chapter B8](https://intro2echo.com/shunts)). In EXPAND, a clip attached to one leaflet only in 1.8% of patients, most often after the posterior leaflet came free[107](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-107).

### Key points

1. TEE guides each step: the bicaval, short-axis and four-chamber views for the puncture, and the commissural and long-axis views with 3D for alignment and grasping[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).
2. The puncture lies high and posterior, about 4.5–5.0 cm above the point where the clip will grasp[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).
3. The arms lie square to the line of coaptation, and both leaflets drape over them and stop moving[97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).
4. Residual MR is graded without PISA, and a mean gradient under 5 mmHg is preferred[80](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-80), [97](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-97).

## Cuestionario del original

### 1. The sheath tents the fossa ovalis. In the four-chamber view, how high above the point where the clip will grasp should the tent be?

1. 2.0–3.0 cm
2. 4.5–5.0 cm
3. 6.0–7.0 cm

**Respuesta del original:** 4.5–5.0 cm

**Explicación:** ASE 2026 gives about 4.5–5.0 cm above the point of grasp, which lies at or a little above the annulus for a flail or prolapse and can lie below it in secondary MR. The bicaval and short-axis views place the tent high and posterior.

### 2. Above the valve, the clip's arms are open and square to the line of coaptation. What does the commissural view (55–75°) show?

1. Both arms at full length
2. The arms edge-on, as one line
3. The arms as a V, open toward the LV

**Respuesta del original:** The arms edge-on, as one line

**Explicación:** The commissural view lies along the line of coaptation, so the two arms, square to it, show edge-on, as one line. The long-axis view (100–160°) shows both arms at full length.

### 3. Two thin jets remain after the clip, one on each side of it. How is the residual MR graded?

1. By PISA and the EROA of each jet
2. By the vena contracta of each jet and the other signs, not by PISA
3. By the sum of the color jet areas

**Respuesta del original:** By the vena contracta of each jet and the other signs, not by PISA

**Explicación:** The clip distorts the hemispheric flow convergence that PISA assumes, and there are often two or more jets, so ASE 2019 and 2026 do not recommend PISA after TEER. A vena contracta of 0.7 cm or more in any jet is specific for severe MR; with several jets, 3D TEE adds their vena contracta areas.

### 4. Before release, the mean gradient across the double orifice is measured. Which result does ASE 2026 prefer?

1. Under 5 mmHg, with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more
2. Under 10 mmHg at any heart rate
3. Any value, if the MR is mild

**Respuesta del original:** Under 5 mmHg, with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more

**Explicación:** ASE 2026 prefers a residual mean gradient under 5 mmHg with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more, reported with the heart rate. In 268 patients, an invasive gradient above 5 mmHg just after implantation predicted a worse long-term outcome.
