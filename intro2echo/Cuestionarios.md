# Todos los cuestionarios conservados en el original

## pulse

### 1. A reflector lies at a depth of 8 cm. When does its echo arrive after the pulse is transmitted?

1. After approximately 52 µs
2. After approximately 104 µs
3. After approximately 208 µs

**Respuesta del original:** After approximately 104 µs

**Explicación:** The round trip takes approximately 13 µs per centimeter of depth, so the echo arrives after 8 × 13 ≈ 104 µs.

### 2. The transmit frequency increases from 2.5 MHz to 5 MHz with 2 cycles per pulse. What happens to axial resolution?

1. It halves (finer detail)
2. It doubles (coarser detail)
3. It does not change

**Respuesta del original:** It halves (finer detail)

**Explicación:** The wavelength halves, so the spatial pulse length and the axial resolution halve. The depth of penetration decreases.

### 3. Why does the lung block the parasternal ultrasound beam?

1. Air absorbs the ultrasound
2. The tissue–air impedance difference causes almost total reflection
3. The pulse is too short

**Respuesta del original:** The tissue–air impedance difference causes almost total reflection

**Explicación:** The acoustic impedance of air is far lower than that of soft tissue, so nearly all of the incident intensity is reflected at the tissue–air boundary.

## image

### 1. An image is acquired at a depth of 16 cm with 100 scan lines and one focal zone. What is the approximate frame rate?

1. Approximately 12 frames per second
2. Approximately 48 frames per second
3. Approximately 200 frames per second

**Respuesta del original:** Approximately 48 frames per second

**Explicación:** Each scan line takes 2 × 0.16 m ÷ 1540 m/s ≈ 208 µs. A frame of 100 lines therefore takes approximately 21 ms, which corresponds to approximately 48 frames per second.

### 2. Which change in the imaging settings increases the frame rate?

1. A greater imaging depth
2. A narrower sector
3. More focal zones

**Respuesta del original:** A narrower sector

**Explicación:** A narrower sector requires fewer scan lines for each frame, so each frame takes less time to acquire.

### 3. A second image of the heart appears behind the bright pleural line. Which artifact is this?

1. Reverberation
2. Mirror image
3. Shadowing

**Respuesta del original:** Mirror image

**Explicación:** The pleura acts as an acoustic mirror and produces a copy of the structures in front of it at a position behind it.

## loop

### 1. Afterload (Ea) increases while contractility (Ees) is unchanged. How does the EF change?

1. It decreases
2. It increases
3. It does not change

**Respuesta del original:** It decreases

**Explicación:** Because EF = Ees ÷ (Ees + Ea), a larger Ea at the same Ees gives a lower EF.

### 2. The slope of which relation is an index of contractility?

1. The EDPVR
2. The ESPVR
3. The Ea line

**Respuesta del original:** The ESPVR

**Explicación:** The slope of the end-systolic pressure–volume relation, Ees, is a relatively load-independent index of contractility.

### 3. In a ventricle with increased chamber stiffness, which change appears first on the pressure–volume loop?

1. A steeper EDPVR, with a higher filling pressure at the same volume
2. A decrease in the ESPVR slope
3. A fall in Ea to zero

**Respuesta del original:** A steeper EDPVR, with a higher filling pressure at the same volume

**Explicación:** Chamber stiffness is a passive property of the ventricle, so it changes the end-diastolic pressure–volume relation.

### 4. Which phase of the pressure–volume loop is absent in chronic severe mitral regurgitation?

1. Isovolumic contraction
2. Ejection
3. Filling

**Respuesta del original:** Isovolumic contraction

**Explicación:** The LV ejects into the LA from the onset of contraction, so LV volume decreases before the aortic valve opens.

**Respuesta del original:** 50%

**Explicación:** The reported velocity equals the true velocity × cos θ, and cos 60° = 0.5.

1. A deeper sample volume
2. A lower Doppler frequency
3. A change to continuous-wave Doppler

**Respuesta del original:** A deeper sample volume

**Explicación:** A deeper sample volume lowers the PRF and therefore lowers the Nyquist limit.

### 3. Why does continuous-wave Doppler not identify the depth at which the peak velocity originates?

3. It does not correct for the beam angle

**Respuesta del original:** It samples every depth along the beam

**Explicación:** Continuous-wave Doppler transmits and receives continuously, so every depth along the beam contributes to the signal.

## pressure

### 1. The LVOT area is 3.1 cm², the peak LVOT velocity is 1.0 m/s and the peak aortic velocity is 4.0 m/s. What is the aortic valve area?

1. 0.78 cm²
2. 1.2 cm²
3. 0.25 cm²

**Respuesta del original:** 0.78 cm²

**Explicación:** The same flow passes both levels: 3.1 cm² × 1.0 m/s = AVA × 4.0 m/s, so the AVA is 3.1 ÷ 4 = 0.78 cm². The velocity ratio, 1.0 ÷ 4.0 = 0.25, also suggests severe stenosis.

### 2. Two patients have the same aortic valve and the same peak aortic velocity. In which one is the Doppler gradient most likely to exceed the catheter gradient from the LV to the aorta?

1. Ascending aorta 2.4 cm
2. Ascending aorta 4.0 cm
3. No difference

**Respuesta del original:** Ascending aorta 2.4 cm

**Explicación:** Past the valve, part of the pressure returns as the jet slows (pressure recovery). The recovery is larger in a small aorta and should be taken into account when the ascending aorta is narrower than 30 mm.

### 3. The peak TR velocity is 3.0 m/s. The IVC measures 2.4 cm at end-expiration and collapses by 30% with a sniff. What is the estimated RVSP?

1. 39 mmHg
2. 51 mmHg
3. 44 mmHg

**Respuesta del original:** 51 mmHg

**Explicación:** The RV–RA pressure gradient is 4 × 3.0² = 36 mmHg. An IVC wider than 2.1 cm that collapses by less than 50% with a sniff gives an RA pressure of 15 mmHg, so the RVSP is 36 + 15 = 51 mmHg.

### 4. When must the velocity proximal to a stenosis be included in the Bernoulli equation?

1. When it exceeds 1.5 m/s
2. Always
3. Never

**Respuesta del original:** When it exceeds 1.5 m/s

**Explicación:** The simplified equation neglects the proximal velocity; above 1.5 m/s, the proximal velocity is too large to be neglected.

## peri

### 1. The end-diastolic echo-free space of a pericardial effusion measures 15 mm. How is the effusion graded?

1. Mild (small)
2. Moderate
3. Severe (large)

**Respuesta del original:** Moderate

**Explicación:** The ESC guideline grades an end-diastolic echo-free space of <10 mm as mild, of 10–20 mm as moderate and of >20 mm as large.

### 2. Which echocardiographic finding is nearly 100% sensitive and specific for tamponade?

1. RA collapse for more than one third of the cycle
2. A brief RA inversion
3. An IVC wider than 2.1 cm

**Respuesta del original:** RA collapse for more than one third of the cycle

**Explicación:** A brief RA inversion also occurs in the absence of tamponade, and a dilated IVC is sensitive but not specific. In the ASE guideline, RA collapse for more than one third of the cardiac cycle is the finding with this sensitivity and specificity.

### 3. In the parasternal long-axis view, fluid lies between the descending aorta and the heart. Which fluid collection is it?

1. Pericardial effusion
2. Pleural effusion
3. Ascites

**Respuesta del original:** Pericardial effusion

**Explicación:** According to the ASE guideline, pericardial fluid lies anterior to the descending aorta, whereas pleural fluid extends posterior to it.

### 4. The septum shifts with respiration, the medial e′ is 12 cm/s and the hepatic vein expiratory diastolic reversal ratio is 0.65. Do the Mayo Clinic criteria indicate constrictive pericarditis?

1. Yes: a septal shift with a medial e′ of 9 cm/s or more
2. No: the reversal ratio is below 0.79
3. No: all three findings are required

**Respuesta del original:** Yes: a septal shift with a medial e′ of 9 cm/s or more

**Explicación:** The Mayo Clinic criteria require a respiratory septal shift together with a medial e′ of 9 cm/s or more or a reversal ratio of 0.79 or more. This combination had a sensitivity of 87% and a specificity of 91%. All three findings together raise the specificity to 97% but lower the sensitivity to 64%.

**Explicación:** Each model outputs a heat map for each point that it looks for, and the public code takes the weighted center of the map as the point.

### 2. A video is exported without its DICOM header. What can the model still do?

1. Find the caliper ends, but not convert them to centimeters
2. Measure in centimeters as before
3. Nothing: it needs the header to find the points

**Respuesta del original:** Find the caliper ends, but not convert them to centimeters

**Explicación:** The points come from the image. The pixel size that converts pixels to centimeters, and the velocity per pixel of a Doppler image, come from the DICOM file.

### 3. A heat map has two peaks of equal height, at the septum and at an RV trabeculation. Where does the point fall?

1. Between the two peaks
2. On the septum
3. On the trabeculation

**Respuesta del original:** Between the two peaks

**Explicación:** The weighted center of two equal peaks lies midway between them, on neither structure, so the wall measures wrong.

## shunts

### 1. Across a restrictive atrial communication, the peak septal velocity is 2.0 m/s, and the RA pressure is 8 mmHg. What is the peak LA pressure?

1. 16 mmHg
2. 24 mmHg
3. 32 mmHg

**Respuesta del original:** 24 mmHg

**Explicación:** The peak LA–RA gradient is 4 × 2.0² = 16 mmHg. The peak LA pressure is the RA pressure + the gradient: 8 + 16 = 24 mmHg.

### 2. Why can the flow across a large secundum ASD not show a raised LA pressure?

1. The flow is right to left
2. The atrial pressures equalize, so the gradient stays at about 1 mmHg
3. CW Doppler cannot record flow across the atrial septum

**Respuesta del original:** The atrial pressures equalize, so the gradient stays at about 1 mmHg

**Explicación:** A large defect lets the LA and RA pressures equalize. In ASD the mean transatrial septal velocity is about 0.4 m/s, a gradient of about 1 mmHg, so the LA pressure is close to the RA pressure. Only a restrictive communication keeps a gradient that gives the LA pressure.

## mteer

### 1. The sheath tents the fossa ovalis. In the four-chamber view, how high above the point where the clip will grasp should the tent be?

1. 2.0–3.0 cm
2. 4.5–5.0 cm
3. 6.0–7.0 cm

**Respuesta del original:** 4.5–5.0 cm

**Explicación:** ASE 2026 gives about 4.5–5.0 cm above the point of grasp, which lies at or a little above the annulus for a flail or prolapse and can lie below it in secondary MR. The bicaval and short-axis views place the tent high and posterior.

### 2. Above the valve, the clip's arms are open and square to the line of coaptation. What does the commissural view (55–75°) show?

1. Both arms at full length
2. The arms edge-on, as one line
3. The arms as a V, open toward the LV

**Respuesta del original:** The arms edge-on, as one line

**Explicación:** The commissural view lies along the line of coaptation, so the two arms, square to it, show edge-on, as one line. The long-axis view (100–160°) shows both arms at full length.

### 3. Two thin jets remain after the clip, one on each side of it. How is the residual MR graded?

1. By PISA and the EROA of each jet
2. By the vena contracta of each jet and the other signs, not by PISA
3. By the sum of the color jet areas

**Respuesta del original:** By the vena contracta of each jet and the other signs, not by PISA

**Explicación:** The clip distorts the hemispheric flow convergence that PISA assumes, and there are often two or more jets, so ASE 2019 and 2026 do not recommend PISA after TEER. A vena contracta of 0.7 cm or more in any jet is specific for severe MR; with several jets, 3D TEE adds their vena contracta areas.

### 4. Before release, the mean gradient across the double orifice is measured. Which result does ASE 2026 prefer?

1. Under 5 mmHg, with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more
2. Under 10 mmHg at any heart rate
3. Any value, if the MR is mild

**Respuesta del original:** Under 5 mmHg, with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more

**Explicación:** ASE 2026 prefers a residual mean gradient under 5 mmHg with a valve area of 2.0 cm² or more, reported with the heart rate. In 268 patients, an invasive gradient above 5 mmHg just after implantation predicted a worse long-term outcome.

## tteer

### 1. Which pair of leaflets can a tricuspid clip grasp to reduce TR?

1. The anterior and posterior leaflets
2. The septal leaflet with the anterior or the posterior leaflet
3. Any two leaflets, with the same effect

**Respuesta del original:** The septal leaflet with the anterior or the posterior leaflet

**Explicación:** The septal leaflet is grasped with the anterior or the posterior leaflet. A grasp of the anterior and posterior leaflets alone has not been shown to reduce TR. Without a focal defect, the anteroseptal line, usually the longest, comes first.

### 2. The arms lie square to the anteroseptal line of coaptation. Which view shows their orientation most accurately?

1. The 3D en face view of the valve
2. The bicaval view
3. The parasternal long axis on TTE

**Respuesta del original:** The 3D en face view of the valve

**Explicación:** The 3D en face view, with multiplanar reconstruction to place the two 2D planes at the site of the grasp, shows the orientation most accurately. The transgastric short axis of the leaflet tips is the other check, with care for off-axis cuts.

### 3. After the grasp, the residual TR must be graded. Which method grades it best with a clip in place?

1. PISA and its EROA
2. The 3D vena contracta area
3. The color jet area in the RA

**Respuesta del original:** The 3D vena contracta area

**Explicación:** With a clip in place, PISA tends to overestimate the residual TR. The 3D vena contracta area grades it best.

### 4. In TRILUMINATE Pivotal, which outcome carried the primary end point at 1 year?

1. Fewer deaths
2. Fewer heart failure hospitalizations
3. A better quality of life (KCCQ)

**Respuesta del original:** A better quality of life (KCCQ)

**Explicación:** Death or tricuspid surgery and heart failure hospitalization did not appear to differ at 1 year. The KCCQ score rose by 12.3 points with TEER and by 0.6 points with medical therapy alone. In the hierarchy, a KCCQ gain of at least 15 points gave 21% of the wins to TEER and 7% to the control group.

## tavr

### 1. Where is the aortic annulus measured on TEE to size a transcatheter valve?

1. At the sinotubular junction, in diastole
2. At the hinge points of the cusps, in systole, inner edge to inner edge
3. At the tips of the open cusps, in systole

**Respuesta del original:** At the hinge points of the cusps, in systole, inner edge to inner edge

**Explicación:** The annulus that sizes the valve is the diameter at the hinge points of the cusps, measured in systole on a zoomed long axis, inner edge to inner edge (ASE 2022). CT is the reference for sizing; 3D TEE measures 3.5–7.4% smaller, usually without a change of valve size (EACVI 2023).

### 2. A 26-mm SAPIEN 3 sits across the annulus before its balloon is inflated. What final depth is the target?

1. 80/20 to 90/10 of the frame's height, aortic to ventricular
2. 50/50: the middle of the frame at the annulus
3. The whole frame below the annulus, in the LVOT

**Respuesta del original:** 80/20 to 90/10 of the frame's height, aortic to ventricular

**Explicación:** A SAPIEN 3 is placed for a final depth of 80/20 to 90/10 of its frame's height, aortic to ventricular (Blackman 2021). The frame shortens as it expands, mostly at its ventricular end, from 27 to 20 mm for the 26-mm valve (Schymik 2015), so that end rises toward the annulus as the balloon fills.

### 3. At the check, two paravalvular jets take 20% of the frame's circumference. What is the grade?

1. Mild
2. Moderate
3. Severe

**Respuesta del original:** Moderate

**Explicación:** The circumferential extent is the sum of the jets' vena contracta arcs divided by the circumference of the valve's outer edge: less than 10% is mild, 10–29% moderate and 30% or more severe, best read with the vena contracta (ASE 2024). A jet counts only where it enters the LV at the frame's ventricular edge; color in the sinuses above the skirt is not a leak (ASE 2019).

### 4. Which result meets the valve's intended performance in VARC-3?

1. A mean gradient of 25 mmHg and a DVI of 0.40
2. A mean gradient of 10 mmHg, a peak velocity of 2.2 m/s, a DVI of 0.46 and mild paravalvular regurgitation
3. A peak velocity of 3.4 m/s with no regurgitation

**Respuesta del original:** A mean gradient of 10 mmHg, a peak velocity of 2.2 m/s, a DVI of 0.46 and mild paravalvular regurgitation

**Explicación:** VARC-3 asks for a mean gradient below 20 mmHg, a peak velocity below 3 m/s, a DVI of 0.25 or more and less than moderate regurgitation (Généreux 2021). Mild paravalvular regurgitation meets it; with the SAPIEN 3, at least moderate regurgitation at 30 days carried a hazard ratio of 2.40 for death at 1 year (Pibarot 2017).
