Resting pressures and flow (catheter)

Pulmonary hypertension is defined by a mean PA pressure above 20 mmHg at rest. The same guideline keeps a wedge pressure of 15 mmHg as the cut-off between pre-capillary and post-capillary pulmonary hypertension. [31](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-31)

Left ventricle and left atrium (2D)

Values are the mean ± 2 SD of healthy adults. LVEF is mildly abnormal at 41–51% in men and 41–53% in women, moderately abnormal at 30–40% and severely abnormal below 30%. The LA volume index is mildly abnormal at 35–41, moderately at 42–48 and severely above 48 mL/m². [14](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-14)

Aortic stenosis

Any one criterion suggests severe stenosis; concordance of all criteria is preferred. Very severe stenosis is defined by a Vmax ≥5 m/s or a mean gradient ≥60 mmHg. [30](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-30), [38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38)

| Grade | MVA (cm²) | Pressure half-time (ms) | Mean gradient (mmHg) | PA systolic (mmHg) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Progressive, mild | >2.5 | <100 | <5 | <30 |
| Progressive, moderate | 1.6–2.5 | 100–149 | 5–9 | 30–49 |
| Severe | ≤1.5 | ≥150 | ≥10 | ≥50 |

Mitral stenosis (rheumatic)

The mean gradient applies at a heart rate of 60–80 bpm. ACC/AHA lists an MVA ≤1.5 cm² and a pressure half-time ≥150 ms for severe stenosis; by MVA = 220 ÷ pressure half-time, 150 ms corresponds to 1.47 cm². [66](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-66), [38](https://intro2echo.com/#ref-38)
