# Vent Lab — Learn the basics

# 01. Why ventilate?

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Understand the purpose and indications of MV

## Pregunta

Why do some patients require mechanical ventilation, and what are the indications?

## Respuesta del original

MV is used when a patient **cannot maintain adequate oxygenation, ventilation, or work of breathing** despite noninvasive support, or when **airway protection** is needed. It supports gas exchange and reduces respiratory muscle load while the underlying process is treated.

Common indications: **refractory hypoxemia, hypercapnic respiratory failure, excessive work of breathing/fatigue, or airway protection.**

The ventilator is not only a supportive therapy, it is also **a tool that gives you information**. Everything in this lab is read from three scalars over time: pressure, flow and volume.

## Actividades

1. Find the three scalars on the screen: **pressure** (yellow), **flow** (teal) and **volume** (green). Freeze the screen with **Freeze** (or F) to look closely.

2. Hover over (or tap, when frozen) a point on the scalars to read its exact values.

# 02. AC/PC vs AC/VC

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Outline AC/PC, AC/VC and modifiable parameters

## Pregunta

What is the difference between AC/PC and AC/VC?

## Respuesta del original

Both are **assist-control** modes in which you set the RR, FiO₂ and PEEP. In assist-control the ventilator delivers a **full preset breath** whether it is triggered by the patient or by the backup rate.

In **AC/VC**, tidal volume is set and **pressure varies**. In **AC/PC**, inspiratory pressure is set and **tidal volume varies**.

You can often name the mode from the scalars alone: AC/VC has a **square (or decelerating ramp) flow** with a rising pressure; AC/PC has a **square pressure** with an exponentially decaying flow. In this curriculum we stick to square and decelerating-ramp flow in AC/VC.

## Actividades

1. Open **MODE** and start **AC/PC**. Notice which scalar becomes square.

2. Go back to **AC/VC** and switch the **Flow pattern** tile to **Decel ramp**.

# 03. What “mode” do we breathe in?

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Outline AC/PC, AC/VC and modifiable parameters

## Pregunta

What “mode” do we breathe in when we breathe spontaneously?

## Respuesta del original

We breathe with **negative pressure** generated by our respiratory muscles. The ventilator provides breaths with **positive pressure**.

If we had to match a ventilator mode to spontaneous breathing, **pressure-targeted modes are more similar** than volume-targeted modes.

2. Keep holding for more than 3 seconds. Your muscles can’t contract forever: the breath ends on its own.

# 06. Oxygenation vs ventilation

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Outline AC/PC, AC/VC and modifiable parameters

## Pregunta

Which parameters affect oxygenation and which affect ventilation?

## Respuesta del original

**PEEP and FiO₂ affect oxygenation.** PEEP works by raising **mean airway pressure**, the average airway pressure across the breath cycle.

**RR and VT (or inspiratory pressure) affect ventilation**, because CO₂ clearance follows minute ventilation (VT × RR).

The ABG panel in the monitor estimates the steady-state gas you would get with the current settings.

## Actividades

1. Increase the **RR** and watch the estimated **PaCO₂** fall.

2. Increase **PEEP** to 12 or more and watch **Pmean** and **PaO₂** rise.

# 08. Trigger, inspiration, cycle, expiration

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Outline AC/PC, AC/VC and modifiable parameters

## Pregunta

Which portions of the breath are trigger, inspiration, cycle and expiration?

## Respuesta del original

**Trigger:** initiates inspiration (the patient, or time based on the RR).

**Inspiration:** flow into the lungs.

**Cycle:** initiates expiration.

**Expiration:** flow out of the lungs. Like a pulled spring that is let go, the lungs recoil inward **passively**.

The phase bands shade inspiration teal. Grey triangles mark time-triggered breaths.

## Actividades

1. Freeze the screen and hover over a breath. Find the moment the flow crosses zero going down: that is the **cycle**.

2. Make inspiration longer (raise **Ti**) and watch expiration get shorter.

# 10. Pressure support

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Review PSV and SBT

## Pregunta

What is pressure support mode, and how does it differ from assist-control?

## Respuesta del original

PSV is a **patient-triggered, pressure-supported** mode in which you set the inspiratory pressure, PEEP and FiO₂. The **RR is solely the patient’s**.

Each breath ends when inspiratory flow falls to a set fraction of its peak (the cycle threshold), so the patient also controls inspiratory time.

# 11. When PSV? What is an SBT?

## Pregunta

In which scenarios is pressure support appropriate, and what is an SBT?

## Respuesta del original

PSV fits **SBTs, weaning, stable patients with a reliable respiratory drive**, or patients intubated solely to protect the upper airway.

# 12. RSBI

Session 1 · Indications, basic modes and scalars

## Objetivo

Review PSV and SBT

## Pregunta

Can you describe the utility of the RSBI?

## Respuesta del original

The **Rapid Shallow Breathing Index** = RR ÷ VT (in liters). It is a bedside screening tool during weaning/SBTs. A lower RSBI reflects more efficient breathing. **RSBI < 105** generally suggests a higher likelihood of successful extubation, but interpret it alongside the clinical status and trajectory.

RSBI appears in the Timing panel in spontaneous modes.

# 14. How does spontaneous effort move air?

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Describe the single-compartment model of the lung

## Pregunta

How does spontaneous inspiratory effort drive air into the alveoli?

## Respuesta del original

The patient generates **negative pleural pressure**, which lowers alveolar pressure “below” airway pressure, and air flows into the lungs. Because the alveolar space is connected to atmosphere, flow occurs so rapidly that **alveolar pressure stays near atmospheric**.

Watch the digital lung: Ppl (pleural, measured by the balloon as Pes) swings well negative while Palv barely dips.

## Actividades

# 17. Peak and plateau

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Classify airway, alveolar and pleural pressure

## Pregunta

What are peak and plateau pressures? How are they measured and what do they represent?

## Respuesta del original

Think of a **straw (airway) and a balloon (alveoli)**. Resistance to flow in a straw rises with its length and gas viscosity, and falls with the **fourth power of its radius** (Poiseuille).

**Peak pressure** combines the elastic load of the balloon and the resistive load of the straw. To measure only the balloon, remove resistance by stopping flow: an **end-inspiratory pause**. The pressure during that pause is the **plateau pressure**, reflecting alveolar/balloon pressure. In ARDS we aim for **Pplat < 30 cmH₂O**.

## Actividades

1. Press **Insp hold**. Read **Ppeak** and **Pplat**; the drop between them is the straw.

2. Raise **Peak flow** to 90 and hold again: the peak rises, the plateau doesn’t.


Fuente: https://www.boardsidereviews.com/vent-lab.html#learn

# 18. Airway, alveolar, pleural

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Classify airway, alveolar and pleural pressure

## Pregunta

How do airway, alveolar and pleural pressures differ in the single-compartment model?

## Respuesta del original

**Airway pressure** is measured at the airway opening. **Alveolar pressure** is the pressure within the alveoli (approximated by Pplat). **Pleural pressure** surrounds the lung and can be estimated with **esophageal manometry**.

**Transpulmonary pressure (PL)** = alveolar − pleural: the distending pressure across the lung itself.

## Actividades

1. Do an **Insp hold** and read **PL end-insp** in the balloon panel (Pplat − Pes).

2. Do an **Exp hold** and read **PL end-exp**.

# 19. When Pplat misleads

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Classify airway, alveolar and pleural pressure

## Pregunta

When is plateau pressure an unreliable surrogate for transpulmonary pressure?

## Respuesta del original

Pplat reflects the **whole respiratory system**: lung plus pleural cavity, chest wall and abdomen. It is unreliable whenever **pleural pressure is abnormal**: active expiration, obesity, or a large pleural effusion. Elevated pleural pressure raises the measured Pplat without raising the distending pressure across the lung.

Two patients with the same Pplat but different pleural pressures can have very different alveolar stress. **Esophageal manometry** estimates pleural pressure and gives a better transpulmonary pressure.

## Actividades

1. Do an **Insp hold**. Pplat looks worrying, but check **PL end-insp**.

2. Do an **Exp hold**: PL end-exp is negative (the lung is being squeezed shut). Raise PEEP until PL end-exp is about 0 or higher, then hold again.

# 20. Stiff balloon: elastic load

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Distinguish the two components of ventilator load

## Pregunta

What if the balloon at the end of the straw is very stiff and difficult to inflate?

## Respuesta del original

An **increased elastic load** (decreased compliance; “elastic load” and “compliance” describe the same concept from opposite sides). Both **peak and plateau rise**, and the **difference between them stays normal** (assuming no added resistive load).

Causes: ARDS, pneumonia, pulmonary edema, atelectasis, fibrosis, auto-PEEP, right mainstem intubation, effusion, pneumothorax, obesity, ascites or abdominal compartment syndrome.

## Actividades

1. Press **Insp hold**. Is Ppeak − Pplat < 10?

What would the scalars look like in pulmonary fibrosis versus asthma on AC/VC?

# 23. Auto-PEEP

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Understand auto-PEEP

## Pregunta

What is auto-PEEP, how do you identify it, and how do you quantify it?

## Respuesta del original

Auto-PEEP comes from **incomplete exhalation**: progressive air trapping and higher intrathoracic pressure, above the set PEEP. It can **impair venous return, reduce cardiac output** and raise the risk of barotrauma. As it worsens, peak and plateau both rise.

**Identify:** expiratory flow **does not return to baseline** before the next breath.

**Quantify:** an **expiratory hold** measures total PEEP; auto-PEEP = total PEEP − set PEEP.

## Actividades

1. Press **Exp hold** and read total PEEP and auto-PEEP.

2. Fix it: change settings until an **Exp hold** shows auto-PEEP ≤ 2.


Fuente: https://www.boardsidereviews.com/vent-lab.html#learn

# 24. Choosing the I:E ratio

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Setting inspiratory and expiratory ratios

## Pregunta

How do we choose an I:E ratio, and what should it be with a high resistive load (status asthmaticus)?

## Respuesta del original

It depends. A **longer inspiratory time raises mean airway pressure** (may help oxygenation) **at the cost of expiratory time** (risk of dynamic hyperinflation).

With an excessive resistive load, prolong expiration: an **I:E of 1:3 or more** (1:4, 1:5) is a reasonable start, then adjust until expiratory flow returns to baseline.

Remember: expiration is passive; you lengthen it indirectly by shortening inspiration or lowering the rate.

## Actividades

1. Reach an **I:E of 1:3 or more** (watch the I:E value).

2. Get expiratory flow back to baseline: confirm with an **Exp hold** showing auto-PEEP ≤ 2.


Fuente: https://www.boardsidereviews.com/vent-lab.html#learn

# 25. Safety features and alarms

Session 2 · Single-compartment model, loads and safety

## Objetivo

Describe and adjust safety features, including alarms

## Pregunta

What safety features are built into the ventilator, and how should alarms be set?

## Respuesta del original

Pressure limits, disconnect/low-pressure detection, apnea/backup ventilation, low minute-ventilation alarms, FiO₂ alarms and high-RR limits: they **prevent barotrauma** and **flag hypoventilation or disconnection**.

Set alarms relative to expected values: **high pressure** above the typical peak (catches obstruction/coughing), **low pressure/disconnect** near baseline, a **minute ventilation/VT floor** for hypoventilation, and an appropriate **apnea time**. Reassess after changes in mode, sedation or mechanics.

## Actividades

1. Set **Pmax** below the current peak pressure and watch the high-pressure alarm cut breaths short.

2. Disconnect the circuit (button below) and watch the alarm.

## Acciones interactivas

- Disconnect circuit
- Reconnect

# 26. Case 1 · hypercapnia

Session 3 · Case-based simulation

## Objetivo

Case 1 · COPD exacerbation

## Caso

**A 65-year-old man with a COPD exacerbation** is intubated for worsening hypercapnia and altered mental status. AC/VC: **VT 450 mL (~6 mL/kg PBW), RR 14, FiO₂ 60%, PEEP 8**.
ABG at 30 minutes: **pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 65, PaO₂ 65** on FiO₂ 60%.

## Pregunta

What is the primary problem, and which setting(s) should you adjust?

## Respuesta del original

**Hypercapnia** (low pH, high PaCO₂). Ventilation follows minute ventilation (VT × RR), so increase one of them. VT is already lung-protective, so a safe change is to **increase the RR**.

Alternatively, with no acute lung injury requiring strict limits, **VT can go to 8 mL/kg PBW provided Pplat stays < 30**. Beware: a higher RR can cause **dynamic hyperinflation** in obstructive disease.

## Actividades

1. Increase minute ventilation (RR or VT) and watch the estimated PaCO₂ improve.

# 27. Case 1 · falling blood pressure

Session 3 · Case-based simulation

## Objetivo

Case 1 · COPD exacerbation

## Caso

A few minutes later the nurse notices the patient needs **escalating norepinephrine**. Look at the scalars.

## Pregunta

Describe what is happening based on the scalars, and how you would fix it.

## Respuesta del original

**Dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP)** from insufficient expiratory time with obstructive physiology. Trapped gas raises end-expiratory alveolar and intrathoracic pressure, **impairs venous return** and causes hypotension. Make sure expiratory flow returns to baseline before each breath.

To lengthen expiratory time:

- **AC/VC and AC/PC:** lower the RR (consider sedation, even neuromuscular blockade, for patient-triggered tachypnea); treat the obstruction (bronchodilators).
- **AC/VC:** decrease VT (if > 6 mL/kg) and **increase inspiratory flow**; both shorten inspiration.
- **AC/PC:** decrease the set inspiratory time.
- An **I:E of 1:3 or more** is a reasonable start; adjust until expiratory flow reaches baseline.

This may worsen the acidosis, but with circulatory collapse looming, it is the least-harm trade.

## Actividades

1. Confirm it with an **Exp hold**.

2. Fix it, then prove it: **Exp hold** with auto-PEEP ≤ 2 and MAP ≥ 65.

# 28. Case 2 · mode and initial settings

Session 3 · Case-based simulation

## Objetivo

Case 2 · ARDS

## Caso

**A 50-year-old woman** with multifocal pneumonia, severe hypoxemia and hypercapnia is intubated; imaging and ABG are consistent with **ARDS**. Height **5′10″ (70 in)**.

## Pregunta

Which mode would you use, and what initial settings?

## Respuesta del original

No mode is inherently superior, but the landmark lung-protective ARDS trials used **AC/VC**, which guarantees low VT and reliable plateau monitoring. AC/PC can work but holding a stable 6 mL/kg needs close monitoring. The true therapy is **lung-protective ventilation**.

- **PBW** for a 70-inch female ≈ **68.5 kg**.
- **VT 6 mL/kg ≈ 410 mL**.
- **RR ~18–24**, titrated to a safe I:E (typically 1:2).
- **PEEP/FiO₂ from the ARDSNet table**, e.g. FiO₂ 80% with PEEP 14. Wean FiO₂ to ≤ 60% as soon as feasible.

## Actividades

1. Choose **AC/VC**.

2. Set **VT ≈ 6 mL/kg PBW** (400–420 mL), **RR 18–24**, **PEEP 14**, **FiO₂ 80%**.

# 30. Case 2 · permissive hypercapnia

Session 3 · Case-based simulation

## Objetivo

Case 2 · ARDS

## Caso

After VT is finalized, the repeat ABG at 30 minutes shows **pH 7.21, PaCO₂ 63, PaO₂ 84** on FiO₂ 80%.

## Pregunta

Should you make any changes?

## Respuesta del original

In ARDS we prioritize low lung volumes and tolerate **permissive hypercapnia** to keep Pplat < 30. With severe hypercapnic acidemia (**pH < 7.20**), **raise the RR first**, reserving VT increases for refractory cases while staying lung-protective.

An excessively high RR can itself be injurious and can cause auto-PEEP. **A normal ABG is not always the goal in ARDS.**

## Actividades

1. Raise the **RR** (not VT) and watch the estimated pH.

2. Check that flow still returns to baseline: **Exp hold** with auto-PEEP ≤ 2.
